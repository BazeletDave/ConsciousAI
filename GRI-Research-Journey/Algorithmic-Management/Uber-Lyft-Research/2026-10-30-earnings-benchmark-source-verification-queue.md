## Methodological reconciliation

Do not average hourly values whose denominators differ. **Engaged/active time** excludes some or all waiting and repositioning; **online time** includes availability; **shift time** can include off app work and unpaid repositioning. A dollar amount per trip cannot be converted to hourly net income without time, mileage and expense records.

Separate (1) gross receipts including tips and bonuses, (2) variable cash contribution after fuel and incremental costs, (3) fully allocated vehicle income after maintenance, insurance, depreciation and attributable finance, and (4) after tax personal income. Taxes and vehicle costs must not be double counted. A tax mileage benchmark is not a measured expense for every driver.

The existing three UberX screenshots and TPA waiting lot narrative are separate evidence records; they do not validate the national earnings claims. Market heterogeneity is a hypothesis requiring comparable samples and denominators.

## November 15, 2026 | Marginal work, benefit eligibility and institutional distribution

**Status:** Bounded GRI analytical extension of the existing longitudinal Uber and Lyft research; prospective economic model, not a verified individual tax or eligibility determination, platform causation finding, or a new research program.

### Research proposition

Increasing app-displayed gross receipts can coincide with diminishing or negative *incremental economic welfare* after vehicle operating costs, tax consequences, benefit changes and foregone alternatives. This is conditional, not universal. Additional hours can remain beneficial; the hypothesis requires person-specific, jurisdiction-specific and time-specific counterfactual evaluation.

### Distinguish the relevant accounting measures

For hours h and documented platform receipts R(h), define cash contribution C(h) = R(h) - incremental fuel, maintenance and other cash costs. Define economic vehicle income E(h) = R(h) - properly allocated operating expenses, depreciation and attributable fixed costs. Then model household disposable resources W(h) = E(h) - applicable taxes + valued transfers and subsidies - out-of-pocket health premiums and costs, with consistent tax treatment and without double counting vehicle deductions. Compare marginal changes W(h+delta)-W(h), not only total gross earnings or app-displayed hourly rates.

**Critical caveats:** Costs can be fixed, variable, stepwise or delayed; maintenance and depreciation must not be double counted. Taxes and eligibility calculations may use different income definitions. ACA Marketplace premium tax credits generally depend on projected annual household income and other eligibility conditions, not gross ride receipts alone; Medicaid eligibility, education aid and other programs have separate rules. Subsidies may phase out or have threshold effects depending on law and year. No general claim that 40 or 50 hours necessarily produces lower net welfare is established.

Test within-driver changes with consistent cost accounting, matched time windows and alternative explanations (demand, seasonality, vehicle age, geography, vehicle type, fuel price and strategic shift selection). Report gross receipts, contribution margin, full economic income and household resources separately. A modeled benefits effect is not a measured causal outcome.

### Institutional political economy and AI Paradox

This extends the established GRI analysis of algorithmic authority and information asymmetry into a **marginal welfare and opportunity-cost paradox**: an optimization interface may emphasize gross activity while the worker's decision depends on net household resources and nonmonetary opportunities. Compare platform-level growth and incentives with driver-level risk and cost allocation, without presuming intentional earnings suppression or a shared algorithm across platforms.

Nelson's institutional political-economy perspectives as attributed analytical lenses: market efficiency and voluntary exchange; strategic public support and infrastructure; bargaining power, cost shifting and distribution. ### Falsification and boundaries

The proposition would be weakened for a specified cohort and period if reconciled marginal income remained positive after documented costs and benefit effects, or if reported thresholds and foregone opportunities were not applicable. A loss of subsidy must be distinguished from the gross value of a subsidy and from net household welfare. Neither an opaque dispatch algorithm nor a negative driver outcome alone establishes intent, manipulation or unlawful conduct.
