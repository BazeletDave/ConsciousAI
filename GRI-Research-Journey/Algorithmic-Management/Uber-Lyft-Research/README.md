Research Overview

Situated within GRI's broader research journey beginning in 2021, this project investigates algorithmic management through longitudinal field observation of Uber and Lyft, examining how digital platforms allocate work, influence compensation, manage labor supply, monitor performance, and structure human decision making.

Drawing on more than a decade of reported rideshare experience, including over 20,000 passenger trips, the research integrates practitioner observations, platform evidence, academic scholarship, institutional analysis, and quantitative economic modeling.

The Approximately $20 Gross Earnings Band

GRI’s October 2026 research examines whether algorithmic pricing and dispatch systems produce an effective gross earnings equilibrium near $20 per displayed trip hour in South Florida.

Three documented Lyft style reservation offers produced:

* $20.54 per displayed trip hour, including a known bonus.
* $19.79 per displayed trip hour, excluding the known bonus.

These preliminary observations do not demonstrate intentional wage targeting, realized driver earnings, or a platform wide compensation policy.

### November 16, 2026 | Longitudinal comparability and changing platform rules

**Methodological control:** A historical trip record establishes an observation at its original time and place; it does not establish that the same pricing, dispatch, incentive or eligibility rule operates in a later period. Client app releases, terms acceptance, platform policy announcements and server-side model or configuration changes are separate events and must not be conflated. An app update is neither necessary nor sufficient evidence of a material change in compensation or dispatch logic.

For existing, lawfully held records, distinguish the observed outcome, contemporaneous disclosed terms, market conditions, transaction type, app version where documented, and authenticated evidence of applicable decision rules. Label unverified policy or model versions **unknown**. Use within-period comparisons or clearly justified cross-period controls before interpreting changes as algorithmic effects. Do not reconstruct missing historical versions, treat individual offers as platform-wide policy, or infer corporate intent from update timing.

**Participation and contestability:** Record separately whether an update was installed, revised terms were presented and accepted, a consequential rule was disclosed, a participant could understand the change, continued platform access was conditioned on acceptance, and an effective review or correction path existed. These conditions require case-specific evidence; a driver's account of practical pressure is relevant testimony but not a universal legal conclusion.

### Existing GRI framework: full economic burden rather than gross fares

Record the same driver's **online time**, engaged trip time, pickup time, unpaid waiting, deadhead miles, gross receipts, rider-paid fares where legitimately available, tips, incentives, toll charges and reimbursements, fuel/charging, maintenance, depreciation, insurance premiums allocated to period, and incident-specific out-of-pocket losses. Report both cash contribution and fully allocated net income. Do not subtract a hypothetical insurance deductible as a realized loss unless a qualifying incident and payment occurred; do not double count tolls, reimbursed expenses, claim payments or vehicle depreciation. Gross platform receipts and driver earnings are distinct measures.

**Candidate observations, not established effects:** Heavy rain may alter demand, supply, cancellation rates, pricing, travel speed, road availability, crash exposure and claims. These pathways can act in opposite directions: reduced supply may increase displayed fares while slower trips and deadhead travel reduce net hourly income.
