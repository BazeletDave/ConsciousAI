# Florida NEVI Funding, Driver Economics and the AI Paradox
**GRI existing Uber and Lyft longitudinal algorithmic management study | Institutional evidence extension | 30 October 2026**

## Verified reporting and qualification

Ashley Miznazi, *Miami Herald*, republished by WLRN July 30, 2026, reports that Florida proposed directing approximately **$197 million** in federal National Electric Vehicle Infrastructure (NEVI) funds originally intended for EV charging toward an **Aerial Highway Network** supporting electric air taxis and associated charging/landing infrastructure. The proposal describes **32 sites**. Source: https://www.wlrn.org/light/environment/2026-07-30/florida-wants-to-use-cash-meant-for-electric-car-chargers-on-flying-cars-instead and https://www.wusf.org/politics-issues/2026-07-30/florida-wants-to-use-cash-meant-for-electric-car-chargers-on-flying-cars-instead . The article says FHWA agreed Florida's highway charging corridor was sufficiently built out; the proposed alternative use was awaiting separate approval at the time of reporting. **Do not describe $197 million as spent, permanently withheld, finally approved or currently frozen without newer official records.** A September 3, 2026 advocacy statement describes the action as a proposal: https://electrificationcoalition.org/tell-florida-leaders-to-keep-ev-charging-funds-on-the-ground/ .

**Institutional positions:** Florida argues the private sector has adequately served conventional EV charging and investment should enable emerging mobility. Critics argue that apartment dwellers and other underserved EV users retain charging gaps and question who benefits from public support for unproven commercial services. These are attributed positions, not GRI findings about policy merit. The public finance question is allocation, opportunity cost, access and oversight, not an assertion of improper conduct.

## Driver economic calculation and limitations

A **hypothetical $6.66 offer for 26 minutes** is **$15.37 per hour on displayed trip time before expenses** ($6.66 / (26/60)). It is **not net hourly earnings**. Add unpaid pickup, waiting, deadheading and return time to the denominator. Subtract fuel, maintenance, tires, depreciation, insurance and attributable costs from receipts. If total occupied plus unpaid time were 40 minutes, gross per total hour would be $9.99 before expenses. The 40 minute illustration is an assumption, not an observed trip.

The existing [Participant 0107 interview](appendix-a-interview-0107-2026-10-23.md) separately records a **$14.15 offer, 33.3 miles, 46 minutes** and illustrative fuel assumptions. Do not conflate the user's $6.66 example with Participant 0107 or claim it is independently documented without locating the underlying trip record.

The [Chicago Lyft paired transaction case](case-study-002-chicago-consecutive-lyft-rides.md) reports **estimated Lyft fees** of $1.99 versus $21.03, but not verified final driver payout. The [Algorithmic Mobility Paradox working paper](algorithmic-mobility-paradox-working-paper-2026-10.md) contains additional driver accounts with unresolved provenance and confounders. These support hypotheses and qualitative analysis, not population prevalence.

The core AI Paradox hypothesis is that individually permissible platform, procurement and infrastructure decisions may interact to produce consequences that are difficult to explain, contest or allocate fairly. This is a research proposition; evidence does not yet establish such an outcome in Florida.
