# Recorded Rides: safety and evidence governance

November 24, 2026 | Bounded extension of existing algorithmic mobility, AI Paradox, Black Box Paradox and Tiger Legacy research.

## Evidence register

TNW (November 23, 2026) reports a Recorded Rides pilot in Baltimore, Raleigh, Indianapolis and St. Louis, using opted-in drivers' smartphone cameras, with encrypted local storage and a 14-day default retention period subject to incident or insurance reporting. This is attributed reporting, not independent GRI reproduction. TNW also cites a 2022 study identifying 22 Uber driver deaths over five years; underlying study not independently verified here. Do not treat this as an annual count or a current risk rate.

GAO-26-108552 (July 24, 2026) independently documents incomplete assault data, driver concerns about recording reliability and access, and limitations of available evidence. Its interviews are nongeneralizable. Uber's 2026 Governance Strategy and Engagement Report identifies Record My Ride as a preexisting safety feature; the new pilot concerns matching, not the invention of smartphone recording.

## Institutional analysis

Apply the existing Nelson-informed institutional lens: distinguish formal consent rules, informal norms, economic incentives, enforcement and actual decision rights. The driver provides hardware and labor while platform policy governs matching and evidence access. Potential additional costs, unequal access and adverse consequences of opting out are hypotheses, not findings. The AI Paradox asks whether assurance capacity keeps pace with new recording capabilities. The Black Box Paradox asks whether affected parties can reconstruct and contest evidence-based decisions. Do not presume AI adjudication or surveillance misuse has occurred.

## Measurement and assurance

| Domain | Proposed measurement |
| --- | --- |
| Safety | Reported incidents per matched trip and driving hour, stratified by city, time and exposure; distinguish changes in reporting from changes in incidence |
| Economic choice | Compare offers, acceptance, net hourly earnings and device costs for opted-in and opted-out drivers; account for selection bias |
| Evidence integrity | Recording failure frequency, authorized retrieval success, retention compliance, provenance and review latency |
| Procedural fairness | Notice, consent withdrawal, access to relevant evidence, appeal outcomes and independent review |
| Privacy and security | Purpose limitation, encryption, access logging, deletion exceptions, minors and jurisdiction-specific consent |

Rival hypothesis: voluntary recording improves deterrence, evidence quality and driver trust without material economic penalty. Compare matched cohorts and pre/post trends, report denominators and uncertainty, and avoid causal claims without credible identification. Tiger Legacy crosswalk is conceptual: consent provenance, access authorization, evidence integrity, retention and independent audit. No control testing or certification is claimed. No collection of passenger footage, personal identifiers, new deployment or new project is authorized. Field observations must remain anonymized and consent governed.
