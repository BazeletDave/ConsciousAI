## Research focus

This case connects the proposed translation of Earl Woods and Pete McDaniel's *Training a Tiger* into a machine learning curriculum with algorithmic management, retail affordability, physical construction, institutional authority, regulation, and continuing executive education.

The central question is whether increasingly effective optimization can outpace an institution's capacity to evaluate consequences, preserve human competence, and correct its governing objectives.

## Contents

The complete case contains an executive briefing, the Synthetic Kinetics Labs decision scenario, implications across markets and institutions, seven research exhibits, an experimental protocol, a teaching note, a 90 day executive learning sequence, and a source register.

## Evidence boundaries

Synthetic Kinetics Labs, Elena Rostova, and the model results are fictional scenario material. The case does not claim that GRI has built a golf robot or demonstrated the stated performance rates. Specific coaching mechanisms require verification against the book. Proposed software modules are not represented as existing repository implementations.

Uber, Walmart, and Promise Robotics materials are identified as corporate statements. Walmart's shelf labels are distinguished from surge pricing. The D.R. Horton agreement is an announced arrangement rather than independently verified production outcomes. Cross domain curriculum transfer remains a research proposition.

This independent GRI case implies no sponsorship or endorsement by Harvard, Thunderbird, ASU, the Woods family, or the companies discussed.
