## Research question

How do social media recommendation systems, paid promotion, account characteristics, social influence and audience engagement relate to the distribution and growth of digital content, and what can be inferred about visibility versus human judgment?

## Protocol for subsequent measurements

1. Preserve the canonical video URL, upload date and complete video file or a reliable archive reference. Do not place copyrighted or personal information in a public repository without permission.
2. Collect native platform analytics screenshots or exports with capture timestamps at a consistent cadence, initially daily for 14 days, then weekly through day 90. Store raw evidence separately; use immutable filenames.
3. Record cumulative views, available impressions, unique reach, audience retention, watch time, likes, comments, shares, saves, follower count, account status, campaign spend, referral source and material editing or cross-promotion events.
4. Record external shocks such as reposts by larger accounts, press mentions, celebrity shares, platform changes, trending topics, and paid promotion of related content.
5. Predefine comparison videos with similar format, duration, publication window and account characteristics where feasible. Compare organic-only posts with clearly labeled paid campaigns only if reliable campaign expenditure and delivery data are available.
6. Track changes from the previous observed count, time-normalized view velocity, and engagement rates using disclosed denominators. Indicate missingness and do not substitute views for unique people.
7. If analyzing comments, deidentify participants and use a documented coding procedure; no inference of changes in beliefs from view counts alone.
8. Version changes to the protocol and keep conclusions separate from observations.

## Hypotheses for future testing

- **H1:** For comparable content, organic visibility growth is heterogeneous and potentially associated with early engagement, audience size and referral patterns.
- **H2:** Paid promotion is associated with a different distribution of impressions and view velocity than nonpromoted content, conditional on measured campaign and content factors.
- **H3:** Shares by high-audience accounts are associated with discontinuous increases in view velocity.
- **H4:** Exposure is not equivalent to persuasion; assessing judgment change requires additional measures such as surveys or ethically designed controlled studies.

All hypotheses remain untested in this case.

## Limitations

Single-case observation, platform-dependent metric definitions, opaque recommendation systems, incomplete exposure data, selection effects and unmeasured external attention prevent causal conclusions. Any observed association must not be presented as evidence of deliberate platform bias or manipulation. This study does not measure minds, persuasion or political preference.
