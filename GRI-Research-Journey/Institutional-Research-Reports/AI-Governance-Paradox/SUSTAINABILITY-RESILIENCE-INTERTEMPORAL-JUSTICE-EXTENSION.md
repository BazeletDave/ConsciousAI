# Sustainability, Resilience and Intertemporal Justice
**David A. Grand | Grand Research Institute | 29 October 2026**
**Status:** Continuing institutional research; conceptual extension and proposed empirical program, not a revision of historical results.

## Research continuity
This note extends the existing AI Governance Paradox and the 29 October 2026 blockchain custody incident study. It does not establish a separate research initiative or change the original report, experiment data or claims. The principal interpretive lens is Roy C. Nelson's institutional political economy; financial valuation and risk questions draw on the corporate finance domain taught by Lena Booth. These are GRI interpretations, not endorsements or incident-specific conclusions by either scholar.

## Core proposition
Cryptographic integrity, financial profitability and technical innovation do not independently demonstrate legitimate authority, equitable access, intergenerational fairness or resilient institutions. Technological progress is institutionally sustainable only insofar as its financing, operation, governance and distribution of benefits and burdens can be maintained and challenged over time.

## Six linked dimensions
1. **Institutional failure:** Which safeguards, incentives and oversight mechanisms failed or remained effective?
2. **Delegated authority:** Who can authorize consequential actions, with what scope, duration, revocation and independent review?
3. **Evidence and accountability:** What is verifiable, by whom, and what remains an inference?
4. **Systemic consequences:** How do disruptions propagate across financial, technological and public institutions?
5. **Intertemporal justice:** How are current losses, future gains, opportunity costs and delayed recoveries distributed across stakeholders and generations?
6. **Sustainability, resilience and equitable technological access:** Who can afford and use critical software and infrastructure, who underwrites innovation, and how quickly can institutions recover while maintaining essential access?

## Comparative cases and careful distinctions
**Binance (October 2026):** Examine preliminary reported exposure, freeze actions, reimbursement commitments, custody controls and actual remediation. Reimbursement is not itself proof of prevention, restored trust or recovered assets. Preserve evolving incident facts and do not infer intentional causation or AI involvement.

**FTX and Anthropic:** Examine the distinction between governance misconduct, bankruptcy estate duties, historical Anthropic investment, actual proceeds of estate sales and counterfactual appreciation. An illustrative 8% holding at a hypothetical $900 billion valuation would imply $72 billion before dilution, share rights, taxes, restrictions and liquidity; FTX no longer holds that historical stake. Counterfactual opportunity value is not a verified asset, legal customer entitlement or evidence that liquidation was imprudent. Compare bankruptcy-date claim valuation, inflation, creditor recovery, timing and allocation of residual upside.

**NASA and SpaceX:** Public procurement can finance privately developed technological capabilities. NASA's 2014 Commercial Crew awards were approximately $2.6 billion to SpaceX and $4.2 billion to Boeing for contracted capabilities, not personal grants to Elon Musk. Evaluate competition, deliverables, intellectual property, public returns, risk transfer, dependency and continuity. Distinguish procurement payments, grants, loans, subsidies and tax benefits rather than combining them into a single 'government funding' figure.

**AI software access:** Compare subscription prices, enterprise licensing, open models, public computing investment, accessibility requirements, connectivity, compute, energy, language availability and skills. Prices alone do not prove exclusion: measure affordability against income, effective availability, quality and opportunity costs.

## Institutional political economy and finance
Apply Nelson's three views as competing explanatory perspectives, not a scorecard: liberalism examines market innovation and private contracting; economic nationalism examines public investment, national capability and sovereignty; structuralism examines concentration of power and unequal risk distribution. Use Booth's finance domain for risk-adjusted valuation, liquidity versus solvency, cost of capital, time value of money, fiduciary obligations and uncertainty. Include environmental externalities and lifecycle resource costs when claiming sustainability.

## Empirical research design
Construct a case-level evidence register recording event dates, jurisdiction, actor, public/private financing type, contractual obligations, ownership, reported and realized losses, time to recovery, access conditions, relevant regulation and source confidence. Separate primary documents from reporting and GRI interpretation.

Suggested measures: public expenditure per delivered capability; competition and cost overruns; user price as a share of local disposable income; access by geography and language; energy and water per unit of service where reliable; concentration of infrastructure; reserve adequacy and redemption delays; recovery time objective; verified restitution percentage; inflation-adjusted versus nominal recovery; false authorization rate; intervention latency; independent audit completeness. Use comparable denominators, baselines, uncertainty intervals and documented missingness.

Test competing propositions:
* P9: Technical assurance and institutional legitimacy are separable and require distinct evidence.
* P10: Public financing can increase access and innovation while also generating private concentration or dependency; effects vary by contract design and market structure.
* P11: Nominal reimbursement and restoration of original economic exposure differ when timing, inflation or asset appreciation matter.
* P12: Systems with independently enforceable authority limits and recovery protocols show shorter simulated containment and recovery times than systems with only local authorization checks.
* P13: Sustainable technological access depends jointly on affordability, infrastructure, skills and institutional continuity rather than price alone.

Do not make causal claims from descriptive case comparisons. For quantitative work, predefine measures, identify confounders, preserve negative results, and distinguish synthetic experiments from field validation.
