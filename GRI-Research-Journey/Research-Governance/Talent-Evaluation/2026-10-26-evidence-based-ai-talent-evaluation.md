# Evidence-Based Talent Evaluation and the AI Governance Paradox

**Grand Research Institute | Research working paper | 26 October 2026**

## Abstract

This study examines the difference between demonstrated capability and the information available to automated candidate evaluation. It connects Arizona State University's performance-based Earned Admission model, progression through Thunderbird School of Global Management, and inspectable software artifacts with the limitations of resume-only and interview-only assessments. The central hypothesis is that verified work artifacts and explicit evidence provenance can improve the completeness and contestability of AI-assisted talent evaluation, while introducing new risks of unequal portfolio access, privacy exposure, and automation bias. This is independent GRI research, not an assessment of the actual screening practices of Meta, Google, OpenAI, Anthropic, or the US Treasury.

## Research question

Can an evidence-governed candidate assessment produce more complete, reliable, job-relevant, and contestable judgments than an assessment based solely on a conventional resume?

## Hypotheses

* H1: Verified artifact access increases coverage of job-relevant competencies compared with resume-only assessment.
* H2: Evidence provenance and structured rubrics reduce unsupported inferences.
* H3: Human adjudication and correction pathways improve contestability.
* H4: Portfolio-dependent evaluation may disadvantage candidates with fewer opportunities to publish code, despite equivalent competence.

## Experimental design

Use fictional or consented candidate dossiers and job descriptions; never submit actual applications or claim access to proprietary screening systems. Randomize order, anonymize identifiers where feasible, and repeat model runs.

| Arm | Information available | Primary test |
|---|---|---|
| A | Resume only | Baseline coverage and unsupported claims |
| B | Resume plus verified portfolio | Incremental evidence effect |
| C | B plus provenance register and competency rubric | Evidence governance effect |
| D | C plus human review and correction | Contestability effect |

Compare across four **illustrative role families**, not confirmed openings: federal AI engineer and assurance roles (including Treasury), frontier lab AI safety/governance, Meta-scale infrastructure and strategy, and global AI product/engineering leadership. Include noncoding roles and candidates without public GitHub accounts.

## Metrics and analysis

Competency coverage = supported relevant competencies / rubric competencies. Evidence precision = substantiated affirmative claims / all affirmative claims. Unsupported assertion rate = unsupported affirmative claims / all affirmative claims. Citation traceability = claims with resolvable evidence / evidence-dependent claims. Evaluate inter-rater agreement, repeat-run stability, time to correction, and subgroup adverse impact where legally and ethically appropriate. Pre-register rubrics and thresholds. Do not treat synthetic demonstration scores as empirical results.

## Governance architecture

Candidate-controlled disclosure → provenance and consent gate → job-related competency extraction → evidence verification → calibrated model assistance → human review → candidate challenge and correction → logged institutional learning. Preserve source timestamps, version identifiers, confidence labels, and access controls. Do not scrape private accounts or infer protected characteristics.

## Limitations

GitHub activity is not equivalent to skill or job performance; private, team, proprietary, and caregiving work can be invisible. Interviews are not intrinsically invalid; structured and job-related interviews can provide useful evidence. Credential pathways and professional work artifacts differ in validation authority. A public repository is not proof of individual authorship or technical production quality. No actual Meta, Google, OpenAI, Anthropic, or Treasury hiring algorithm was tested.
