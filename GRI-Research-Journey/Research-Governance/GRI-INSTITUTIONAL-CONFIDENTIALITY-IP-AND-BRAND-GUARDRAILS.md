# GRI Institutional Confidentiality, Intellectual Property and Brand Guardrails
**Owner:** Grand Research Institute (GRI)  
**Applies to:** Tiger Legacy, Training a Tiger, Tiger Assurance, Kiongozi, AI Paradox and the broader GRI research portfolio.  
**Classification:** Public governance principles only. This document intentionally excludes confidential implementation details.

## Governing standard
GRI is transparent about its approved mission, research methods, governance principles and appropriately supported outcomes. Transparency does not require disclosure of confidential intellectual property, institutional strategy, business models, objectives, research roadmaps, unpublished results, commercial plans, partner information or independent assessments.

All nonpublic institutional materials are confidential by default. Publication, external sharing, deployment and client disclosure require explicit, appropriately scoped authorization from GRI and any relevant rights holders. Previously approved public material does not automatically authorize disclosure of related private material.

## Institutional history and transition to governed openness
GRI's research, intellectual contributions and professional relationships substantially predate the current GitHub publication program. Historically, the Institute has exercised selective confidentiality over unpublished scholarship, research methods, strategic work, correspondence and professional connections. A substantial body of research was not uploaded to GitHub. The absence of material from a public repository does not imply that it did not exist, that it was unimportant, or that GRI relinquished its rights or confidentiality.

GRI is now selectively making appropriate work more visible to enable reproducibility, scholarly critique, accountable governance, responsible collaboration and potential commercialization. This is **governed research openness**, not blanket disclosure or retroactive publication authorization. GitHub is a curated public research and engineering interface, not a comprehensive archive of GRI's history or private records.

References to scholarship, academic instruction or professional interactions involving professors, RAND, Brookings or other institutions must distinguish independently verifiable public sources from confidential interactions. Do not imply formal affiliation, employment, sponsorship, endorsement, partnership, access to nonpublic institutional materials or institutional approval without supporting evidence and permission. Academic rigor is demonstrated through accurate citations, transparent methods and evidence boundaries, not disclosure of private correspondence or relationships.

Before migrating historical work into public repositories, apply an explicit review for intellectual property ownership, third-party rights, contractual restrictions, personal information, client confidentiality, strategic sensitivity, academic integrity, provenance and disclosure approval. Where a public summary serves the research purpose, publish the summary rather than underlying confidential source material. Retain nonpublic originals in controlled storage with appropriate access and provenance.

**Institutional principle:** GRI can increase transparency about what its research establishes while retaining discretion over how the work was developed, who contributed confidentially, and which strategic directions remain private.

## Academic independence, attribution and protected research synthesis
GRI is an independently directed research institution. Its founder's personal graduate study, academic performance, graded assignments and scholarly engagement may inform GRI research, but do not establish university, faculty, student, colleague, RAND, Brookings or other think tank sponsorship, participation, endorsement or validation of GRI research or products. Academic grades assess coursework under course criteria; they are not independent security certifications or endorsements of GRI platforms. Personal academic records and private educational communications are confidential unless the individual expressly approves disclosure.

Public scholarship that informs a published claim must be attributed accurately with appropriate citations and respect for copyrights and licenses. GRI need not publicly reveal its internal synthesis of scholarly frameworks, unpublished research mappings, proprietary implementation methods, research dependencies, commercial objectives or confidential correspondence merely to establish rigor. When a claim cannot be substantiated publicly without disclosing protected information, narrow the claim, publish an authorized high-level description or arrange appropriately controlled documentary verification.

Do not publish confidential assignments, student contributions, instructor feedback, private research discussions or third-party unpublished materials without necessary rights and permissions. Do not imply affiliation or endorsement based solely on reading an author's publications, attending classes, receiving academic grades or maintaining professional connections.

Describe Training a Tiger, Tiger Assurance, Kiongozi and Tiger Legacy at an approved conceptual level. Preserve confidential engineering, strategic linkages, competitive differentiation and client-specific implementation. Avoid claims of market-leading or unmatched cybersecurity capability without independently substantiated comparative evidence.

## Mandatory controls
1. **Information classification:** Label materials public-approved, internal, confidential or restricted. Restrict confidential and restricted data to approved recipients and purposes; minimize collection and retention.
2. **Intellectual property:** Protect source code, architecture, models, datasets, inventions, research methods, unpublished hypotheses, trade secrets, licensing strategies and provenance. Record ownership and third-party license obligations; avoid posting confidential material in public repositories, CI logs, issues or artifacts.
3. **Institutional strategy:** Protect mission execution plans, strategic objectives, competitive positioning, business and revenue models, budgets, partnerships, client negotiations, investment plans and future initiatives.
4. **Brand governance:** Preserve GRI attribution and approved names for Tiger Legacy, Training a Tiger, Tiger Assurance, Kiongozi and other programs. Do not imply that a brand name is legally registered, exclusive or endorsed by another institution without verification. Review naming, trademark availability and licensing before commercial use.
5. **Research integrity:** Identify hypotheses, synthetic tests, CI verification, independently reviewed evidence and production deployment separately. Do not assert certification, independent validation scope or operational effectiveness beyond authorized supporting evidence.
6. **Independent assessments:** Approved public statement: **“Independent assessment completed. Scope and conclusions available for documentary verification. All independent assessments and supporting evidence remain fully confidential.”** Release of any underlying assessment requires explicit authorization and an appropriate confidential verification process.
7. **Client and partner separation:** Do not disclose client identities, private audits, code, vulnerabilities, commercial terms or access credentials without express authorization. Client approval for testing is bounded by written scope, lawful authority and agreed data handling.
8. **AI and automation boundaries:** Agents and automated workflows must not publish, transmit, train on external services using, deploy or grant access to confidential information without explicit authorization and appropriate technical controls. Preserve human approval, access controls, auditability, revocation and least privilege.
9. **Publication review:** Before any public commit, dashboard change, demonstration, paper or client deliverable, check information classification, ownership, academic attribution, licensing, security exposure, brand accuracy, evidentiary claims and disclosure approval.
10. **Exceptions and incident response:** Record disclosure approvals and exceptions privately. Escalate suspected exposure; revoke access, preserve evidence and assess notification/remediation obligations under applicable law and agreements.

## Operational release gate
**Default: do not publish or share when classification, ownership, client authorization or evidentiary basis is uncertain.** Obtain specific human approval before changing that state. Maintain a confidential evidence register separate from public repository materials.

This governance policy is not itself a technical enforcement mechanism or legal determination. Implement controls through repository permissions, branch protection, secret scanning, CI disclosure checks, confidential storage, contractual terms and independent legal review where appropriate.
