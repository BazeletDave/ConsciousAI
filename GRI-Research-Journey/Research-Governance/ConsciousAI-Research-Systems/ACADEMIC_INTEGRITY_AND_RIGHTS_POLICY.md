# GRI Academic Integrity, Copyright and Research Ethics Policy

**Owner:** Grand Research Institute (GRI), independent research organization founded by David Grand.
**Scope:** Public repository, research datasets, AI agents, dashboards, reports and publications.
**Status:** Internal operational policy; not legal advice or an institutional compliance certification.

## Hard restrictions
1. Do not commit or redistribute full or substantial copyrighted Harvard Business School cases, Harvard Business Review articles, licensed course packets, instructor teaching notes, examination questions, answer keys, graded submissions, classroom recordings or protected course slides without documented rights.
2. Do not ingest these materials into public training sets, public RAG indexes or third-party model services without verifying the applicable license and institutional policies.
3. Public academic references may include accurate bibliographic metadata, links, short quotations where legally permitted, and independently written GRI analysis. Public availability alone is **not** a license to reproduce or train on content.
4. Do not present independent GRI work as endorsed, sponsored, supervised or certified by Thunderbird School of Global Management, Arizona State University, Harvard Business School, Harvard Business Review or faculty members.
5. Attribute faculty-originated models and published scholarship; distinguish these from GRI hypotheses, operational prototypes and empirical findings.
6. Do not publish participant-level interviews, driver trip identifiers, screenshots containing personal data, or confidential employer/platform information without valid consent, minimization and rights review.
7. Do not present a self-reported reviewer attestation as verified independent review, or heuristic evidence scores as proof of factual accuracy.
8. Any uncertainty about copyright, course licensing, academic rules, human-subject protections or authorization means **hold publication** and seek appropriate permission or institutional guidance.

## Mandatory release review
Before merging research content into a public release, a designated human steward records: source and rights basis; permitted use; attribution; confidentiality/consent; academic-integrity check; institution-endorsement check; evidence status; and explicit publication authorization. Missing documentation means **not approved**.

## Automated guardrails and limitations
Automated checks can identify some risky filenames, metadata or phrases, but cannot establish fair use, license validity, consent, copyright ownership, or compliance with university policies. Human review is mandatory. Do not upload restricted source text merely to run an automated scanner.
