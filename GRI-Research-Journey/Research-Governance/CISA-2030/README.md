# CISA 2030 | Institutional Resilience and Autonomous AI Governance

Grand Research Institute | Independent working research program | 28 October 2026

**Status:** Research design and source mapping, not empirical findings, agency endorsement, operational certification, or a deployed dashboard module.

## Research question
Under what conditions can critical infrastructure organizations demonstrate that autonomous systems preserve cybersecurity, accountable human authority, continuity, and contestability simultaneously?

## Hypotheses
H1: Passing technical security checks does not alone establish meaningful human intervention capability.
H2: Provenance completeness and independent replay improve auditability, but do not alone prove legitimacy or safety.
H3: Recovery performance and human override effectiveness are distinct dimensions of institutional resilience.
H4: Cross-border dependencies alter the distribution of authority and recovery costs.

## Comparative cases
1. **Critical software security and PHP remediation:** vendor advisories, version evidence, patch authorization and runtime verification; distinguish synthetic tests from deployed systems.
2. **Uber/Lyft algorithmic management:** documented platform disclosures and consented research observations; driver perceptions are not proof of internal dispatch rules.
3. **Keys AI Connect:** proposed autonomous mobility and infrastructure scenarios; no operational deployment claims.
4. **Institutional accountability:** IFC Performance Standard 1 as a comparative governance analogy, not a cybersecurity standard.

## Experimental design
Pre-register threat model, test environment, sampling, failure criteria and competing explanations. Run offline synthetic scenarios first. For each test record attempted action, policy decision, human authorization, evidence digest, reversal outcome, recovery time and residual harm. Publish negative results and limitations. Do not test third-party production infrastructure without authorization.
