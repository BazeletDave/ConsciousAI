## Why this belongs in the research program
The GRI mobility project asks whether an economically useful, driver-controlled AI planner can protect educational participation while improving the choices available to working students. The ASU statement articulates an institutional objective—accessible, affordable education and measurable student success—that can be translated into researchable questions. The key insight is not that ASU endorsed the project; it is that educational access depends on supporting institutions beyond the classroom, including labor platforms, time allocation, transportation, household resources, and digital decision systems.

## Proposed institutional causal chain
**University access policy and scholarship opportunities -> enrollment and educational commitments -> platform-work scheduling and income constraints -> driver-controlled planning recommendations -> observed time, economic, fatigue and educational outcomes -> institutional feedback and potential policy adaptation.**

At each step identify: formal rules; actors and incentives; effective decision rights; evidence of implementation; accountability and recourse; possible unintended effects. This is GRI's independent extension of institutional political economy taught by Roy C. Nelson, supplemented by leadership and trust considerations from David Slocum and Mansour Javidan and financial opportunity-cost analysis informed by Lena Booth. No faculty endorsement is implied.

## Pilot research questions
1. Does constraint-aware shift planning reduce conflicts between classes, study blocks, rest and paid platform work compared with drivers' ordinary planning?
2. What is the total economic cost of educational participation for working drivers, including unpaid repositioning, foregone earnings, and travel?
3. Does presenting a no-drive option and uncertainty-aware forecasts improve driver agency and planning confidence?
4. Are effects different for scholarship recipients, other working students, and nonstudent drivers after accounting for selection effects and privacy?
5. Which university and platform institutions can responsibly support access without directing drivers' private employment decisions?

## Possible ASU-connected study: conditions precedent
- Seek appropriate institutional research/IRB determination before recruiting students or collecting study-specific human-subject data.
- Participation must be voluntary; do not presume scholarship status authorizes recruitment, access to student records, or special treatment.
- Use separate explicit consent for any trip records, course schedules or interviews; minimize educational and location data.
- No student or driver should be disadvantaged for declining participation; no linkage to grades, scholarship standing or platform access.
- Publish aggregate or deidentified results only when disclosure risk has been assessed.
- Keep public university policy statements separate from GRI's hypotheses and from observed pilot outcomes.

## Potential measures
- **Education:** class attendance conflicts, self-reported study-hour preservation, missed deadlines (consented and minimally recorded).
- **Economics:** realized net contribution per committed hour, unpaid miles, tolls, opportunity costs, earnings variability.
- **Wellbeing/safety:** sleep opportunity, return-home adherence, fatigue exclusions, nighttime driving limits.
- **Agency and accountability:** recommendations overridden, explanation usefulness, consent withdrawal, appeal/correction mechanisms.
- **Model quality:** forecast calibration, scenario feasibility, false-positive profitable-trip recommendations, missingness.
