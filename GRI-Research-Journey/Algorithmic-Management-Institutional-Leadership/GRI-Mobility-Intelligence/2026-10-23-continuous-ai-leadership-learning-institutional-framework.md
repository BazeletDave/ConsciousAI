# Continuous AI Leadership Learning as Institutional Capacity
**GRI Research Extension | October 23, 2026 | Independent research | Working framework, not peer reviewed**

## Thesis
As AI accelerates and increasingly executes consequential organizational decisions, institutions must expand the capacity of people to understand data, exercise judgment, allocate authority, and correct errors. Education is not merely access to an AI interface; it is sustained institutional capability for governance. This is a testable GRI proposition, not an established empirical finding.

## Decision-learning loop
**Data → probabilistic prediction → human judgment → authorized action → measured outcome → institutional feedback.**
For every stage document the actor, authority, evidence, uncertainty, costs, error pathway and recourse. Attractive dashboards do not establish decision quality; education must improve interpretation, oversight and adaptation.

## Proposed research questions
RQ1: Does targeted AI/data/decision-literacy education improve independently scored decision quality for executives, managers and working students?
RQ2: Do explicit decision rights and correction pathways moderate the relationship between AI use and beneficial outcomes?
RQ3: How do time, fatigue, income and study constraints mediate the translation of educational access into student success?
RQ4: Can the GRI driver planner improve net economic contribution while respecting hard sleep, class and return-home constraints?
RQ5: How do different institutions distribute AI benefits and error burdens across firms, workers, customers and students?

## Testable hypotheses
H1: Decision-literacy training plus an auditable decision process improves quality more than tool access alone.
H2: Traceable authority and appeal mechanisms reduce remediation time compared with opaque automated recommendations.
H3: Driver-controlled constraint-aware planning reduces schedule conflicts without increasing fatigue compared with a baseline heuristic.
H4: Benefits differ by institutional resources and access; scholarship status alone does not establish causation.

## Research design
Use a staged mixed-methods protocol: public-document coding, consented interviews, working-student schedule/offer panels, rules-based driver planner, then prospective comparison of decision outcomes. Preregister primary endpoints; distinguish pilot feasibility from effectiveness. Analyze economic net contribution per committed hour, study-block preservation, missed return deadlines, sleep opportunity, driver autonomy, model calibration, and subgroup disparities. A separate enterprise-leadership module can use standardized decision vignettes and independent scoring. Avoid conflating education, software adoption and causal value creation.
