# Transport Automation Resilience: Comparative Evidence Note (22 November 2026)

## Scope
Bounded extension of existing GRI AI Paradox, autonomous mobility, and critical infrastructure governance research. These cases are independent; no common cause or cyber intrusion is inferred.

## Case A: Downtown Miami autonomous mobility
Date: 22 November 2026 (reported). Location: NE 5th Street at Biscayne Boulevard, Miami.
Evidence: user-supplied eyewitness account and street photograph. Account reports three Waymo taxis immobilized, police response, tow truck access difficulties, and passenger egress amid event traffic. Photograph depicts Waymo-branded vehicles and congestion but does not independently establish immobilization, number of affected lanes, duration, cause, or police/towing actions.
Classification: eyewitness-reported; image-supported scene; operational cause unverified.
Open verification: independent traffic/police reports, Waymo statement, incident timing, duration, vehicle count, recovery records, and documented public costs.

## Case B: Delta Air Lines operational automation disruption
Date: 22 November 2026. Confirmed: Delta weight-and-balance automation issue delayed flights nationally during Thanksgiving travel. Manual workarounds were used. FAA said Delta delays were not caused by FAA air traffic systems. AP reported that the automated system was restored later, although residual disruption persisted. Delay counts are time-dependent snapshots, not a fixed incident total.
Classification: confirmed operational disruption; cause beyond reported system issue not independently established here.
Sources:
- Associated Press, 22 November 2026: https://apnews.com/article/d4bdb732da930205ec3453d35c28daa2
- Los Angeles Times / AP, 22 November 2026: https://www.latimes.com/world-nation/story/2026-11-22/automation-system-issue-causes-delta-flight-delays

## Related but separate cases
The earlier FAA nationwide ground stop and United Airlines system interruption provide comparative context only. They must be independently sourced and analyzed separately before assigning causal or quantitative claims.

## Comparative analytical dimensions
1. Dependency concentration and operational single points of failure.
2. Detection, escalation, and accountable human intervention.
3. Safe degraded operation and verified fallback capability.
4. Propagation into shared infrastructure and third-party externalities.
5. Recovery time, passenger access, public response resources, and auditable incident reporting.

## Measurement protocol
Record incident timestamp, source, evidentiary confidence, impacted service units, disruption duration, fallback availability, responder involvement, independently evidenced cost, and resolution status. Treat unknowns as unknown. Separate direct costs, operator costs, and social externalities. Avoid conflating temporal correlation with shared causation.

## Research proposition
Automation can increase throughput while shifting the burden of exceptional recovery to human operators and public institutions. This is a testable governance hypothesis, not a conclusion demonstrated by these two incidents.

## Assurance guardrails
No unsupported cybersecurity attribution. No invented dollar costs. No names of passengers or bystanders. Preserve original user-supplied media outside public repository unless explicitly authorized. This note contains no original photo.
