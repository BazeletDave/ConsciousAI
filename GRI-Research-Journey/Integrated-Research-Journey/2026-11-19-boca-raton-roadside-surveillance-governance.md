# Boca Raton roadside ALPR governance: bounded field observation (19 November 2026)

**Program:** GRI Integrated Research Journey / AI Paradox / institutional cybersecurity and assurance. **Status:** Preliminary observational and documentary comparison; not an equipment identification, legal finding, system audit, or new research program.

## Evidence and provenance

A GRI researcher supplied two photographs on 19 November 2026 and reported encountering similar roadside installations throughout Boca Raton, Florida. The images appear to show a roadside camera assembly with solar power and ancillary hardware. The precise street locations, installation dates, device manufacturer, operator, operational status, network connectivity, and whether either pictured device performs automated license plate recognition (ALPR) have **not** been independently verified. The photographs remain in the source conversation; no identifiable vehicle or location metadata is republished here. Repeated observations are not a verified citywide census.

## Documentary baseline

* Florida Statutes § 316.0777 defines ALPR and provides FDOT discretionary authority over installation within State Highway System rights of way, including removal following notice. It also governs disclosure of certain records and references the retention schedule under § 316.0778. Primary statute: https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/316.0777 (if the section link changes, consult the official 2026 Chapter 316 compilation: https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/Chapter316/All).
* FDOT's 31 August 2026 memorandum revoked permits for ALPR installations in state highway rights of way, requiring removal within 30 days and halting new approvals in its jurisdiction. This is not a general prohibition on city, county, or privately sited cameras. Reporting: https://www.wlrn.org/law-justice/2026-09-01/fdot-revokes-permits-for-flock-cameras-installed-on-state-land-next-to-roads and https://abcnews.com/US/florida-remove-flock-cameras-state-highway-roads/story?id=136118376.
* According to Boca Post's 1 September 2026 report of Boca Raton Police Services statements, the department reported **262 readers** (mostly vehicle mounted), **27** in FDOT rights of way scheduled for removal, **14** stationary readers on city poles or city rights of way, and **95 days** of data retention. These are **reported agency figures**, not an independently audited November inventory. Source: https://bocapost.com/florida-news/fdot-revokes-flock-camera-permits-florida-state-roads/.
* The 30 September removal deadline has passed, but documentary evidence reviewed for this note does not establish whether each of the 27 affected readers was removed, nor whether any photographed installation was among them.

## Comparative institutional assurance matrix

| Control domain | Publicly documented | Evidence required for assurance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Legal authority and siting | State right of way restrictions and local jurisdictional distinctions | Geolocated inventory, road ownership and permit disposition, removal verification |
| Data minimization and retention | Police reported 95 day retention | Written retention schedule, configuration evidence, deletion logs and exception handling |
| Access control | Statutory permitted disclosure categories | Roles, authentication controls, case linkage, least privilege, independent access log review |
| Sharing and vendor governance | Legal framework for criminal justice disclosure | Contracts, interagency agreements, access lists, onward sharing restrictions and vendor subprocessors |
| Algorithmic accuracy | ALPR technology defined by statute | False positive testing, alert confirmation procedures, human review and correction/contest pathway |
| Cybersecurity | Physical equipment observed, no technical audit | Asset inventory, patch policy, encrypted transport, credential management, incident reporting |
| Public accountability | Regulatory action and agency statements | Public policy, periodic audits, misuse investigations, aggregate utilization and outcome reporting |

## AI Paradox hypothesis and limitations

**Hypothesis:** Surveillance acquisition and network reach may grow faster than the institutions' demonstrable ability to govern access, data lifecycle, error correction and oversight. The Boca Raton case supplies a testable governance question, **not proof of systemic abuse, illicit tracking, cybersecurity compromise, or regulatory noncompliance**. Benefits in locating stolen vehicles or missing persons should be evaluated alongside privacy, proportionality, error rates and auditability.
