# Nelson Model | Methodological Boundaries for GRI Research

**Status:** Independent GRI methodological note, 22 October 2026. Not an endorsement by Thunderbird or its faculty.

## Purpose
GRI uses an institutional political-economy lens to examine how rules, actors, interests, power, policy choices and implementation capacity shape algorithmic and agentic management. This is a research method, not a claim that every case has the same causal structure.

## Original teaching example: currency devaluation risk
A slide titled *Assessing Prospects for Financial Crisis* presents seven diagnostic conditions for currency devaluation: (1) fixed versus flexible exchange rate; (2) large versus small current-account deficit; (3) large versus small budget deficit; (4) small versus large foreign reserves; (5) large versus small foreign debt; (6) high versus low political risk; and (7) large versus small contagion effect. The first listed condition is a heuristic, not an unconditional claim that all fixed exchange rates fail or that flexible rates prevent crises. The slide is a user-provided teaching reference; its provenance, date and permitted reproduction rights should be established before embedding the image itself.

These indicators are **currency-crisis indicators**, not an AI risk score, nor an exhaustive formal specification of the broader institutional approach. Do not relabel them as native cybersecurity, autonomous vehicle or LLM indicators.

## GRI comparative extension (proposed; requires empirical validation)
| Institutional question | Candidate AI-specific observable | Limitations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who holds effective authority? | Documented decision rights and agent permissions | Permission lists alone do not prove actual behavior |
| What constrains decisions? | Applicable rules, contract terms and override controls | Jurisdiction and enforceability vary |
| What creates dependence? | Vendor concentration, API dependency and switching costs | Requires system inventory and counterfactuals |
| How resilient is the institution? | Tested recovery times, rollback evidence and independent attestation | Reported uptime does not prove integrity |
| Can risks propagate? | Verified downstream dependencies and incident blast radius | Do not assume contagion without evidence |
| Who can contest outcomes? | Appeal, audit and remediation pathways | Policies must be tested in practice |
| How does adaptation occur? | Time from observed failure to authorized corrective change | Temporal association is not causation |

These are **GRI hypotheses and proposed measures**, not original Nelson financial indicators and not yet validated predictive instruments.

## Application protocol
1. Define the research question, jurisdiction, institution, period and unit of analysis.
2. Identify rules, actors, interests, power and competing explanations from primary evidence.
3. Select domain-appropriate measures; never transplant financial indicators literally into unrelated technical domains.
4. Distinguish verified observations, practitioner accounts, technical demonstrations, analogies and untested hypotheses.
5. Specify falsifiable propositions, missing evidence, uncertainty and limitations.
6. Preserve scholarly attribution without implying university endorsement.

## Priority GRI case applications
- Uber platform management: dispatch, incentives, contractual control, worker agency and contestability.
- Agentic commerce: delegated purchasing authority, permissions, human approval and accountability.
- Cybersecurity restoration: privileged automation, verification of patch state, residual exposure and independent sign-off.
- Autonomous mobility: safety-critical observation integrity, oversight and institutional response.

**Editorial rule:** Use “Nelson Model” only when a specific institutional mechanism or indicator is actually analyzed. Otherwise name the relevant technical, legal, economic or empirical method directly.
