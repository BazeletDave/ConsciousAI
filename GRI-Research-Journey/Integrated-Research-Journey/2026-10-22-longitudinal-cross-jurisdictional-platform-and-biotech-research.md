# GRI Research Journey | Longitudinal Foundations and Institutional Inquiry
**Research synthesis:** 22 October 2026  
**Status:** Independent, evolving research synthesis; not a claim that all historical materials have been independently verified.

## Scope and continuity
GRI research draws on decades of practical experience, early entrepreneurial and organizational leadership, international business, applied scientific and non-scientific observation, collaborative management education, and subsequent research into algorithmic management and AI governance. It did not begin with one company, transaction, technology or academic assignment.

The archive connects earlier experiences to later questions **without treating recollections as independently validated scientific evidence**. Personal experience can motivate hypotheses and case selection; experimental, documentary and comparative claims require appropriate independent evidence.

## Comparative research threads
- **Early practitioner foundations:** Direct commerce, team management, organizational incentives, institutional practices and leadership; historical documents and dates to be verified before public attribution.
- **International practice and regulated industries:** Comparative institutional environments, technology commercialization, differing jurisdictional permissions and constraints. Describe patterns without identifying former private counterparties, partners or confidential projects.
- **Global platform research:** Uber and other mobility systems, international market variation, multi-country experience, collaborative global management work, pitch decks, SWOT and comparative analysis. Original team contributions must be attributed appropriately and shared only with permission. Similar business functionality does not prove copying of code.
- **GRI research since 2021:** Institutional legitimacy, the AI Paradox, algorithmic and agentic management, evidence integrity and governance.
- **Current executable research:** Assurance and bounded-governance prototypes, with reproducible tests and explicit limitations; no unverified claims of autonomous ML validation.

## Historical confidentiality and third-party protections
Public research materials must not name, imply misconduct by, or reveal confidential relationships involving former partners, colleagues, counterparties, investors, organizations or their jurisdictions unless the information is independently public, relevant, verified and approved for inclusion. No proprietary scientific, commercial, contractual or personal material should be uploaded. Avoid suggestive descriptions that make private individuals identifiable by combination of clues.

Keep private source provenance and any permitted restricted archive separate from public-facing GitHub. An author's public biography does not authorize disclosure of third-party records. Existing public repository materials should be reviewed separately for legacy identifiers; this entry does not claim they have been removed.

## Graduate-level research standard
Each substantive research extension should include (1) a clear research question; (2) theory and institutional framing, with the Nelson Model when pertinent; (3) source provenance and dates; (4) a distinction between firsthand observation, collaborator work, hypothesis, documentary evidence and empirical result; (5) methodology and competing explanations; (6) reproducible technical artifacts where relevant; (7) limitations, uncertainty and ethical considerations; and (8) a cross-reference to earlier research. GRI's independent work must not imply endorsement by Thunderbird or faculty.

## Research-to-code traceability
For new systems and algorithms, document the historical antecedent, research question, design rationale, evidence inputs, test results, operational boundary, and future validation plan. Do not retroactively rewrite old artifacts; append dated interpretations and preserve original provenance.
