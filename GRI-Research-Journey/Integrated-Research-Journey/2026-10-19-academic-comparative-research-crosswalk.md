## Management literature

Harvard Business Review is a valuable source of management analysis, but an HBR article is not automatically peer reviewed research. Relevant works to read and assess include:

- Michael Luca, Jon Kleinberg, and Sendhil Mullainathan, “Algorithms Need Managers, Too” (2016): https://hbr.org/2016/01/algorithms-need-managers-too
- Roberto Tallarita, “AI Is Testing the Limits of Corporate Governance” (2023): https://hbr.org/2023/12/ai-is-testing-the-limits-of-corporate-governance
- Vegard Kolbjørnsrud, Richard Amico, and Robert J. Thomas, “How Artificial Intelligence Will Redefine Management” (2016): https://hbr.org/2016/11/how-artificial-intelligence-will-redefine-management

Pair management essays with peer reviewed studies, original company documentation, primary legal and policy records, and independent evaluations. Do not reproduce licensed teaching cases or paywalled articles.

## Method and integrity

For each comparison, record the primary source, publication date, research design, jurisdiction, organizational context, decision rights, outcomes, rival explanations, and evidence limitations. Distinguish documented facts, founder experience, academic instruction, exploratory GRI observations, and original hypotheses. A technical control's compliance with its policy does not alone establish legitimacy or accountability.
