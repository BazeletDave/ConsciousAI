**Grand Research Institute | 18 November 2026**

## Research proposition

AI infrastructure investment may expand more quickly than the financial and institutional capacity to independently evaluate its returns, allocate its risks, and correct investment decisions. This is a **testable hypothesis**, not a finding of an AI financial crisis. The mechanism extends the existing AI Paradox question of whether optimization reshapes its own economic and institutional feedback environment.

Corporate free cash flow, worker economic income and public net benefits are separate measures, not additive without a reconciled accounting perimeter.

Evaluate public subsidy clawbacks, creditor protections, supplier alternatives and worker remedies as distinct controls. ### November 26, 2026 | Mechanism challenge and financial governance stress tests

**Continuity and status:** Build on the capital formation and capital participation sections above. The central proposition is conditional: capital formation can expand productive capacity while the contractual allocation of decision rights and residual returns determines who participates in the gains and who absorbs losses. Challenge the mechanism rather than assume that technological scale, private profitability, public value or distributed ownership imply one another.

| Mechanism | Test and counterfactual | Evidence required / disconfirmation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scale versus concentration | Compare incremental cost and utilization against feasible competitive or modular alternatives; assess switching costs and entry barriers separately | Audited segment costs, capacity, contracts and market definition; lower cost with credible alternatives weakens a dependency claim |
| Capital discipline versus commitment escalation | Recalculate prospective incremental NPV excluding sunk costs; compare continuation, staged investment, abandonment and redeployment | Board-authorized budgets, cash flows, milestone results and alternatives; timely cancellation of uneconomic projects weakens escalation hypothesis |
| Public risk versus private returns | Compare procurement or development support against realistic alternative delivery and no-project scenarios; include contingent liabilities and public service benefits | Actual disbursements, contractual risk allocation, delivery and public opportunity costs; competitive verified public value weakens uncompensated risk transfer hypothesis |
| Equity participation versus control | Separate issued shares, transferred shares, beneficial interests, voting rights and liquidity; compare net household outcomes against diversified savings alternatives | Executed transfers, custody rules, share rights, fees and longitudinal retention; wider effective decision rights or reduced wealth disparities would qualify concentration hypothesis |
| Automation versus labor participation | Compare matched tasks, hours, net income, productivity and new task formation before and after adoption, using suitable controls | Worker and enterprise records, task mix, demand and expenses; sustained gains in net labor income or bargaining power weaken displacement hypothesis |
| Common leadership versus correlated dependency | Map legally documented counterparties, guarantees, shared suppliers and decision authority; test independent failure and joint stress scenarios | Dated filings and contracts, redundancy and recovery evidence; demonstrable operational separation weakens contagion hypothesis |

Separate operating losses, equity impairment and creditor losses to avoid double counting. Measure ROIC minus WACC on a consistent capital base; assess cash runway and covenant headroom separately. Where probabilities cannot be supported, report scenario ranges rather than fabricated expected values. For public investment use a separately justified social discount rate and distributional analysis; transfers between stakeholders are not automatically net social costs. Do not aggregate Musk-associated entities or compare sovereign fund AUM to corporate revenue.

**Governance fundamentals:** For each consequential decision identify the legal entity, principal, delegated agent, mandate, incentive, beneficiary, residual risk bearer, review authority and remedy. The OECD corporate governance principles and agency theory already cited above supply normative and analytical benchmarks. Distinguish legitimate entrepreneurial discretion from demonstrable governance failures. Apply the existing Nelson institutional alternatives, Booth capital budgeting lens and Guthrie organizational learning context without claiming faculty endorsement. The existing Uber/Lyft observations supply labor-side questions, not evidence of causal effects from unrelated capital transactions.

**Evidence gate:** Maintain separate statuses for reported commitment, verified primary disclosure, authorized transaction, executed settlement and measured outcome. For each claim retain source date, jurisdiction, legal entity, accounting perimeter, relevant denominator and unresolved contradictions. External references in preceding sections require independent retrieval before promotion to verified evidence; this subsection introduces no new external factual claims. Tiger Legacy contributes the principle of reconstructable decisions and documented exceptions, not certification. No new repository, dashboard, collector, automated workflow or academic endorsement is proposed.

**Synthesis:** The AI Paradox is not a prediction that automation inevitably concentrates wealth. It is a testable tension between productive capacity, access to economic returns and the institutional ability to contest consequential decisions. Financial viability, equitable distribution, resilience and legitimate authority are separate outcomes that may improve or deteriorate together or independently. Retain negative results and counterexamples; do not select evidence solely because it supports the paradox.

**Identification discipline:** Use within-market and within-period comparisons where feasible; matched transactions do not alone establish causality. **Institutional synthesis:** Financial capital authorizes investment, but legal and managerial mandates authorize decisions. Apply the existing Tiger Legacy assurance principle to evidence traceability, not to claims of certification.
