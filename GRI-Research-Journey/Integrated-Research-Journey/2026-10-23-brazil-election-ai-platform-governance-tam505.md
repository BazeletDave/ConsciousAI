# Brazil 2026 | AI, Electoral Institutions and Platform Governance

**Grand Research Institute | Research Journey | October 23, 2026**  
**Status:** Exploratory comparative institutional case; allegations not independently established  
**Academic foundation:** TAM 505 Global and Regional Environments, taught by Professor Roy C. Nelson at Thunderbird School of Global Management. GRI's application is independent research, not faculty or university endorsement.

## Research question

How do electoral institutions respond when private platform moderation, AI-generated political media and alleged influence technology potentially change access to political information, and what can this reveal about institutional accountability in algorithmic management?

## TAM 505 institutional analysis

The institutional approach taught in TAM 505 by Professor Roy C. Nelson examines how rules, organizations, interests, power and historical context structure strategic choices. GRI applies the following analytic sequence:

1. **Worldview and country context:** Brazil's political economy, democratic competition, regulatory setting and institutional history.
2. **Rules:** Electoral law, court authority, platform moderation rules, transparency obligations and rights of expression.
3. **Actors:** Campaigns, voters, platform operators, journalists, civil society, electoral authorities and prosecutors.
4. **Interests and incentives:** Campaign visibility, user engagement, platform compliance, procedural fairness, reputation and electoral legitimacy.
5. **Power and information:** Private control over ranking, moderation, distribution and data; public capacity to demand evidence and review decisions.
6. **Options, commitment and adaptation:** Auditable notices, appeals, independent investigation, proportionate remedies and institutional learning.

The analytic focus is on mechanisms and contestable evidence, not on favoring either political party or predicting an election.

These are **institutional analogies**, not evidence that Uber and Meta use the same algorithms, policies or causal mechanisms.

## AI Paradox interpretation

**GRI hypothesis:** When private technical capabilities for optimizing allocation, visibility or influence expand faster than accessible evidence and oversight, institutional legitimacy can become harder to establish even when the underlying system is not shown to be unlawful. This hypothesis requires comparative testing and counterexamples.

**Framework:** Access → Capability → Authority → Consequences → Feedback.  
A research program should distinguish system capability, actual deployment, measurable effects, authorized decision rights, procedural review and remediation.
