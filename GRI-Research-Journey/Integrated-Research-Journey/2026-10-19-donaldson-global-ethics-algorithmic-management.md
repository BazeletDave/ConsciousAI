## Executive synthesis

Donaldson's framework provides a historically grounded business ethics lens for GRI's independent research on the AI Paradox, algorithmic management and institutional governance in the Fourth Industrial Revolution. Donaldson does **not** discuss contemporary machine learning or propose the AI Paradox. The transfer to AI governance is **GRI's interpretation** and must be tested rather than attributed to Donaldson.

The governing challenge is how organizations preserve fundamental human protections while respecting cultural and institutional differences in the design, deployment and oversight of algorithmic systems. Optimization of a local metric is not a sufficient test of ethical legitimacy.

## What Donaldson argues (source based)

1. **Reject both ethical extremes.** Donaldson critiques unqualified cultural relativism (“when in Rome”) and ethical imperialism that simply exports home-country practices without attention to local contexts (pp. 4–6).
2. **Three guiding principles.** Respect core human values, respect local traditions, and recognize that context matters in ethical judgment (p. 6).
3. **Core ethical threshold.** He identifies respect for human dignity, respect for basic rights, and good citizenship as foundational values. Companies must translate them into specific operational practices (pp. 7–8).
4. **Moral free space.** Not all divergent business practices are inherently wrong; some allow legitimate local discretion within boundaries set by core values. Donaldson distinguishes conflicts of relative development from conflicts of cultural tradition (pp. 9–11).
5. **Two diagnostic tests in cultural tradition conflicts.** Donaldson asks whether successful business in the host country is possible without the practice, and whether it violates a core human value; his proposed permissibility test requires negative answers to both (p. 11). This is his normative proposal, not a statutory safe harbor.
6. **Ethics must be operational.** Codes unsupported by consistent leadership, concrete procedures, supplier conditions and avenues for local input become empty declarations. His examples include Motorola, Levi Strauss, and cross-border business practices (pp. 8–12).
7. **Moral imagination and institutional reform.** He recommends creative remedies and collective action against corruption rather than treating difficult ethical tradeoffs as merely compliance questions (p. 12).

**Source limitations:** The paper dates from 1996. Its historical cases, country-level generalizations, legal descriptions, numerical estimates and examples should not be repeated as current conditions without independent verification. The original source is copyrighted and course licensed; cite bibliographically and paraphrase rather than reproduce its full text.

## GRI's independent analytical extension to AI systems

| Donaldson's construct | GRI interpretation for AI governance | Possible observable test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core human values | Minimum safeguards for dignity, privacy, nondiscrimination, safety and meaningful challenge to consequential algorithmic decisions | Audit appeal accessibility, safety incidents, disparate impacts and correction time |
| Respect for local traditions | Participatory design and evaluation of AI tools across languages, institutions and cultural settings without assuming fixed national identities | Compare local stakeholder input, usability and contestability |
| Context matters | Validate models in the actual organizational, regulatory and socioeconomic environments where they operate | Compare performance, harms and overrides across sites |
| Moral free space | Allow bounded local configuration where it does not compromise fundamental rights or applicable law | Document decisions on permissible local variation and reasons |
| Ethical leadership | Translate AI principles into operational controls, escalation, independent review and accountable decision rights | Audit whether stated policies match observed procedures |
| Moral imagination | Explore safer alternatives when optimization creates conflicting stakeholder outcomes | Compare remedial designs and stakeholder outcomes |

These correspondences are *research hypotheses and governance recommendations*, not conclusions demonstrated by Donaldson's article.

## Case connections in GRI's existing research

**Platform management (Uber/Lyft).** An efficient dispatch or incentive policy may change worker behavior and the observed data used for subsequent allocations. The ethical question includes whether drivers can understand and contest consequential decisions and whether local labor and regulatory context changes the effects. No proprietary platform mechanism is inferred.

**Financial decision systems.** Risk classification may be statistically useful yet conflict with dignity, rights, transparency or procedural fairness. Research should assess local regulatory duties, documentation, adverse effects and human review.

**Treasury/regulatory label verification.** A classification such as Match, Review Required or Mismatch is a decision aid, not independent legal authority. Ambiguity must have a documented human adjudication path.

**Digital legacy and memory.** Voice synthesis and AI reconstructed narratives require consent, authentic source provenance, clear synthetic labeling, culturally responsive bereavement practices and withdrawal or correction mechanisms. Commercial product capabilities and GRI consulting relationships must not be confused with verified ethical certification or ownership of third-party IP.

**Persistent AI archives.** Retrieval systems should distinguish a historical source, an author interpretation, a generated summary and a later correction. The user can authorize selective access without granting blanket access to all private files.

## The AI Paradox: a normative research proposition

**Proposed GRI proposition:** As AI driven optimization becomes more effective at shaping organizational outcomes, it may also reshape the information and behavior used to justify subsequent decisions. Institutional governance must therefore evaluate both *performance* and *ethical legitimacy*, with attention to local context and nonnegotiable protections.

**Rival explanations:** Outcome changes may result from demand, regulation, demographic composition, measurement error, software updates or institutional practices independent of optimization. Comparative case studies must distinguish these before making causal claims.

**Research questions:** (1) Do operationalized ethical safeguards reduce harmful downstream effects of adaptive algorithms? (2) Under what conditions does local adaptation improve legitimacy without weakening basic protections? (3) Can provenance, contestability and independent validation prevent self-reinforcing errors in digital memory and institutional decision workflows?

## November 6, 2026 | Cultural interpretation and legitimate algorithmic authority

**Placement and scope:** Bounded refinement of this existing Donaldson / AI Paradox analysis, not a new project, empirical result, model capability claim, or change to the public research navigation. The motivating academic comparison is Erin Meyer's *The Culture Map* (2014), GLOBE's cultural and leadership dimensions (House et al., eds., 2004), and intercultural communication concepts discussed in graduate coursework. Private lecture slides, recordings, graded work and course handouts are not reproduced here. Neither the authors nor Thunderbird/ASU endorse GRI's extension.

**Refined GRI research question:** Under what conditions can an AI-mediated management system interpret culturally contextual communication accurately enough to inform consequential organizational decisions, while preserving meaningful human authority, contestability, and core rights?

**Analytical distinction:** Linguistic accuracy is not evidence of accurate inference of intention; accurate inference of intention is not authorization to decide. High- and low-context communication, directness of negative feedback, GLOBE in-group collectivism, humane orientation and power distance are distinct constructs, not interchangeable national labels. Context, workplace role, individual preferences, language proficiency and situational stakes can moderate their relevance. A model must not infer consent, willingness or disagreement from nationality, silence or nonverbal cues alone.

**Proposed hypotheses, not findings:**
1. Systems relying on explicit text alone may misclassify polite refusals, indirect disagreement or deference in some contexts relative to a context-aware, clarification-capable baseline.
2. A culturally adaptive interface may improve user comprehension and perceived procedural legitimacy, but could also increase stereotyping, unwarranted inference or inconsistent treatment.
3. Human clarification and appeal pathways may reduce consequential errors even when an AI system's inferred intention remains uncertain.

**Possible bounded evaluation within existing assurance methods:** Construct rights-cleared, synthetic, culturally ambiguous workplace scenarios with multiple plausible interpretations and benign controls. Compare (A) literal-text interpretation, (B) contextual interpretation with uncertainty reporting, and (C) contextual interpretation with respectful clarification and human review. Pre-register scenario selection, outcome definitions and failure criteria; use culturally competent independent reviewers and retain disagreement rather than forcing consensus. Evaluate interpretation error, unjustified certainty, clarification effectiveness, wrongful consent inference, appeal accessibility, subgroup coverage and documented human override. Distinguish synthetic performance from operational effectiveness. Do not use private coursework as a public dataset or modify frozen Tiger Legacy baselines.

**Institutional safeguards:** Respect locally meaningful communication practices within Donaldson's core-human-values constraints. Require clear notice of AI mediation, appropriate consent, privacy protections, non-discrimination, and a human decision-maker for consequential action. Cultural adaptation must never become a rationale for bypassing an explicit refusal or denying individual rights.

**Rival explanations and limits:** Differences may reflect translation quality, organizational incentives, status, personal communication preferences, language proficiency, interface design or measurement artifacts rather than culture. Existing Uber/Lyft field evidence cannot establish cultural causal effects. No cultural competence benchmark, participant study, independently reproduced result or deployed feature is claimed here.
