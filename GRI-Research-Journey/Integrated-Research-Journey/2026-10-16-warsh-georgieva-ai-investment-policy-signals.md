## Research observation
In the author-provided passage, Warsh describes a policymaker's effort to 'look out the window' rather than rely exclusively on backward-looking economic releases or uncertain forecasts. He discusses the periodic FOMC decision process, international policy spillovers, the difference between secular stagnation and investment-led structural growth, the role of macroeconomic policy and microeconomic institutions, and inflation pressures that coexist with investment growth. Georgieva then introduces uneven global growth associated with enthusiasm for AI. These are paraphrased research observations from a partial supplied excerpt, not a certified verbatim transcript.

## GRI original analytical extension
**The AI policy observation paradox:** monetary and development institutions may need to govern rapidly evolving AI-driven capital formation with lagged measurements, uncertain forward scenarios and uneven international exposure. Investment may simultaneously create short-term demand, electricity and financing pressures and longer-run productivity capacity. Policy impacts cross borders, and unequal access to energy, compute, finance and resilient institutions can widen the global AI divide even while aggregate investment rises.

**Nelson institutional political economy (GRI application):** liberalism frames international investment and coordination; economic nationalism frames domestic industrial, energy and technology priorities; structuralism frames capital concentration, debt constraints and asymmetric ownership. These perspectives are applied independently by GRI, not attributed as new statements by Professor Roy C. Nelson.

**Landry Signé connection:** Signé's public scholarship on the global AI divide foregrounds unequal compute, electricity, supply chains, institutional participation and strategies for inclusive development. This event supplies a contemporary macroeconomic policy counterpart; it does not prove any causal claim in Signé's work.

**World Bank / IMF / resilience:** test how historical development finance and electricity reliability affect the ability of countries to capture AI investment benefits, with debt sustainability, price stability, grid resilience and climate exposure as moderators. Distinguish IMF macroeconomic financing from World Bank project financing. ## Empirical study design
Research question: Can investment, grid load, compute capacity and capital allocation data provide useful leading indicators of AI-driven structural transformation beyond lagged GDP, CPI and employment reports?

Candidate observables: announced vs realized data-center capex, equipment orders, electricity interconnection queues, grid capacity, industrial electricity tariffs, semiconductor imports, labor productivity, wage distributions, CPI/PPI, credit conditions, sovereign spreads, IMF AIPI, World Bank project financing and IFC/MIGA disclosures. Require source vintage, geography, units, ownership, licensing and independent reviewer.

Test rival explanations: ordinary business cycle recovery, non-AI investment, energy price shocks, fiscal stimulus, changes in financial conditions, selection into data-center locations and forecasts later revised. No model performance or causal result is claimed.
