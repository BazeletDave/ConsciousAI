## Research purpose and method

This index maps **specific publicly available faculty-authored works** to bounded GRI research questions. Links indicate analytical relevance, **not faculty participation, endorsement, supervision, review or empirical validation**. Books and publisher articles are distinguished from peer-reviewed empirical articles and institutional faculty biographies. Some sources are publisher descriptions or abstracts rather than full-text critical reviews; those limitations are noted. The GRI connections are proposed interpretations, not claims made by the authors.

The founder's executive practice informs research questions; Thunderbird academic study informs disciplinary interpretation; published sources provide traceable intellectual context; GRI case evidence and reproducible tests determine whether the resulting claims survive scrutiny.

## Cross-cutting AI Paradox research questions

1. **Authority and accountability:** Can a model follow its programmed rules while the institution lacks a defensible chain of responsibility? Compare Nelson, Guthrie and Ear; test against GRI's explicit authorization and evidence requirements.
2. **Human judgment and algorithmic optimization:** When do predictive or prescriptive decisions displace the judgment needed to handle uncertain or exceptional circumstances? Compare Slocum and Serrato; evaluate bounded simulations separately from real-world observations.
3. **Distribution and legitimacy:** Who can participate in developing, auditing and benefiting from AI systems, and who bears their costs? Compare Signé and Booth; examine institutional incentives and financing constraints.
4. **Evidence and assurance:** What evidence would distinguish a technically functioning control from an institutionally legitimate governance arrangement? Apply the GRI assurance framework with independently reproducible tests, documented exceptions and clear scope limitations.
