**Integrative proposition:** As institutional knowledge becomes executable, the ability to act can expand faster than the institution's ability to authorize, understand, contest, correct and independently verify consequential actions. The AI Paradox concerns this potential divergence and the feedback effects of optimization, not an assumption that every automation fails.

## Institutional analysis

The relevant mechanism is codification of discretionary knowledge into reusable instructions, followed by delegated execution, observation, authorized correction and redistribution. Institutional memory becomes an operational input. This is not equivalent to model weight retraining or evidence of machine consciousness.

Applying Roy C. Nelson's institutional political economy lens independently, identify actors (authors, platform owners, reviewers, employees, drivers and affected parties), interests (efficiency, income, control, safety and legitimacy), rules (permissions, review and appeal), effective power (objective setting, access to evidence and corrective authority), and capacity (review resources, remedies and independent verification). Nelson's original currency crisis indicators are not transplanted into AI evaluation.

The existing faculty scholarship crosswalk situates this analysis in Nelson's *Harnessing Globalization* (2009, Penn State University Press) and Doug Guthrie's *Dragon in a Three-Piece Suit* (1999, Princeton University Press). Their empirical settings are distinct from Uber; the extension to AI governance is GRI's own. Organizational rationalization can increase consistency while leaving substantive accountability unresolved.

## Comparative proposition: internal agents and external platform workers

The Black Box Paradox concerns information asymmetry and the ability to challenge decisions. Internal observability is not equivalent to external transparency. The corporate disclosure motivates a comparison of the correction opportunities available to internal AI workflow owners and external human platform workers. It establishes neither the absence of driver remedies nor a measured disparity.

Test whether access to reasons, evidence, human escalation, correction latency and effective remedies differs between comparable consequential decisions, controlling for contractual status, jurisdiction, privacy, task risk and organizational role. Use matched cases where feasible; denominator is all eligible disputed decisions, not only successful appeals. A finding of comparable or better worker remedies would weaken the asymmetry proposition. Missing observations remain unknown.

## Evaluation and economics

Separate task outcome, execution trajectory, delegated authority, evidence integrity and institutional accountability. High task success can coexist with unauthorized action; procedural compliance can coexist with incorrect outcomes. Measure unauthorized allowances and false denials, severe hidden changes, end to end legitimate completion, revocation effectiveness and review overhead using the existing protocol. Stratify by observation quality and consequence severity. Rare consequential failures require targeted controls; aggregate pass percentages are not safety thresholds.

Evaluate net institutional value as avoided task costs plus attributable benefits less integration, maintenance, evaluation, human review, remediation and residual risk costs. Distinguish firm savings from worker income and social costs; no valuation, displacement estimate or enterprise return is asserted. An alternative explanation is that effective versioning, escalation and human review allow governance capacity to keep pace with execution capacity. This is a meaningful competing hypothesis.

### Human and organizational dimension

Treat employment relationship, work location and team cohesion as separate variables rather than mutually exclusive groups. Internal employees may experience reduced repetitive work alongside monitoring pressure; platform workers may face opaque allocation and limited access to decision makers; remote employees may face isolation or enjoy autonomy; cohesive teams may use automation to support cooperation or may suppress dissent through conformity. These are competing mechanisms, not observed population rankings.

Distinguish interpersonal trust, institutional trust and calibrated reliance on machines. Psychological safety concerns speaking up about mistakes and uncertainty, not the absence of performance standards. Cross cultural leadership requires attention to context, power distance, communication norms and trust formation; do not infer an individual's preferences from nationality. Preserve human dialogue and escalation alongside automated reporting. Compare internal workflow owners and platform workers without treating agents as human rights holders or assuming identical legal relationships.

### Synthesis

The four dimensions are technological capability, institutional authority, human experience and economic value. Capital finances infrastructure; infrastructure enables delegation; delegation reorganizes work and feedback; human and institutional consequences determine legitimacy and sustainable value. Feedback from each layer can reshape the others. The central question is whether these capacities coevolve, not whether machines replace relationships in every workplace. The 1990s practitioner antecedent, 2021 onward research, 2025 author publication and dated 2026 academic and engineering records provide a chronology, not proof of historical priority. This is an integrated extension of the existing GRI milestone; no new project, engineering, dashboard deployment, independent certification or peer review.
