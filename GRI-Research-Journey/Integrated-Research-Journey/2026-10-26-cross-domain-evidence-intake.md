## Priority candidate study: Algorithmic adaptation in platform labor

**Question:** Under what conditions do worker strategies for declining low value assignments alter platform allocation, incentives, or economic outcomes?

**Competing hypotheses:**  
H1: Increased rejection of low value offers is associated with changes in subsequent offer compensation, controlling for geography, time, demand, and supply.  
H0: No measurable relationship remains after those controls.  
H2: Observed changes reflect ordinary market conditions or dispatch mechanisms rather than strategic coordination.

**Potential measures:** offer value, estimated net hourly earnings, distance, unpaid repositioning, acceptance and rejection rates, offer sequence, time of day, demand conditions, and documented platform policy changes.

**Design:** Prospective, consent based observation with preregistered definitions, anonymization, comparison groups, and sensitivity analysis. Do not infer platform source code or intentional manipulation from anecdotal screenshots. Do not test using unauthorized accounts, coordinated manipulation, or policy violations.

**Ethical constraints:** Migrant status is not an explanatory factor absent a specific, lawful, ethically reviewed research question and reliable evidence. Do not identify couriers from the screenshots. Respect platform terms, privacy, labor protections, and jurisdictional requirements.

## Cross domain governance mapping

- **Access:** Who can obtain platform work, advanced computing, biometric systems, or expert knowledge?
- **Capability:** What operational or predictive power does the technology create?
- **Authority:** Who is empowered to make, contest, or override consequential decisions?
- **Consequences:** How do labor outcomes, privacy, economic distribution, and institutional legitimacy change?
- **Feedback:** How do affected people and institutions adapt, and how is this reflected in system governance?
