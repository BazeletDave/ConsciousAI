# Energy, Development Finance, and the Global AI Divide
## A GRI Research Journey institutional resilience case study

### Research thesis
The global AI divide is not solely an adoption or digital literacy gap. It may also reflect decades of unequal access to affordable reliable energy, development finance, grid resilience, computing infrastructure, investment capital, and institutional decision-making authority. GRI asks how historical infrastructure finance and governance shape AI opportunity, technological dependency, local value capture, and resilience.

### Institutional finance mechanisms
- **World Bank / IBRD / IDA:** historical project-level energy and grid financing, investment outcomes, access, reliability and implementation; separate commitments, disbursements, completion and impact.
- **IMF:** fiscal space, balance-of-payments constraints, debt sustainability, macroeconomic policy and AI Preparedness Index; IMF should **not** be misrepresented as a general direct project lender for power plants or data centers.
- **IFC / MIGA:** private project investment, safeguards, guarantees, sponsor incentives, stakeholder engagement and distributional risks.
- **Energy and technology sources:** IEA, World Bank WDI, national utilities, regulators, grid operators, IMF AI Preparedness Index, and documented data-center capacity inventories. Distinguish proposed from operating capacity.

### GRI analytical pathways
1. Historic financing, energy policy and investment → electricity cost, reliability and grid capacity.
2. Electricity and capital constraints → feasibility and location of domestic compute, data centers and digital infrastructure.
3. Ownership, regulation, institutions and labor capabilities → AI adoption and distribution of economic value.
4. Climate shocks, grid redundancy and recovery capacity → resilience of AI services and communities.
5. External compute and finance dependence → potential sovereignty and accountability tradeoffs.

**Competing outcomes:** infrastructure can enable local AI opportunity, or concentrate returns with external owners while raising local energy/water pressures. Neither outcome should be presumed without evidence. Imported cloud services and smaller efficient models may reduce domestic infrastructure requirements.

### Research questions
- Does historical energy investment predict subsequent AI readiness after controls for income, education, institutions and connectivity?
- When do development finance and public-private arrangements retain benefits locally?
- How do grid resilience and climate exposure modify the relationship between compute access and operational AI capability?
- Can shared compute, regional coordination and distributed energy help countries reduce AI dependency?
- Which of Nelson's institutional perspectives best explains different country outcomes, and under what conditions?

### Metropolitan Park, Queens | Urban leapfrogging and accountable digital infrastructure (1 November 2026)

**Scope:** An extension of the existing GRI energy, resilience, institutional political economy, AI Paradox and Kiongozi research. This is a prospective, public-source urban case, **not** a new paper, GRI partnership, technology deployment, or independently validated evaluation of Metropolitan Park.

**Verified development context:** The New York State Gaming Commission awarded the Metropolitan Park gaming license in December 2025. The development team's published vision concerns approximately 50 acres surrounding Citi Field and includes public space, hospitality, entertainment and transport improvements. October 2026 reporting documents continuing Public Design Commission scrutiny, including accessibility, stormwater and public-space concerns. A license does not imply every design approval is complete, nor that proposed digital technologies are installed. Sources: [New York State casino applicant executive summary](https://nycasinos.ny.gov/metropolitan-park-executive-summary); [Metropolitan Park project FAQ](https://www.metropolitanpark.com/faq); [October 2026 public design reporting](https://tollbit.gothamist.com/news/new-renderings-are-out-for-metropolitan-park-casino-queens); [October 2026 design concerns](https://patch.com/new-york/queens/8-1b-queens-casino-hits-new-roadblock-despite-winning-license).

**GRI research thesis:** Metropolitan Park is a bounded opportunity to study whether redevelopment in a mature megacity can leapfrog fragmented legacy infrastructure through integrated digital services while preserving public rights, resilience and equitable access. “Leapfrogging” here is a testable analogy, **not** a claim that Queens faces the same development conditions as the Global South. Landry Signé's published research on the global AI divide emphasizes electricity, compute, supply chains and institutions; GRI independently applies those questions to an urban district. **Nelson institutional political economy crosswalk (GRI interpretation):** Liberalism asks whether interoperable vendors and investment partnerships increase choice and innovation; economic nationalism asks how public authorities secure local jobs, infrastructure and strategic capability; structuralism asks who controls data, capital, surveillance systems and the distribution of returns. These are independent applications of established academic perspectives, not statements attributed to Professor Nelson.

**AI Paradox and Kiongozi tests:** (1) Faster automated admission might increase total attendance and congestion; (2) frictionless identity checks might create exclusion or surveillance costs; (3) AI-managed queues might optimize venue metrics while shifting burdens to transit users or workers; (4) integrated vendor systems might improve service while increasing common-mode cyber failure; (5) robot labor and voice agents might improve availability while reducing human recourse. All are hypotheses. For each, record baseline, intervention, affected groups, competing explanation, human override, adverse effects, independent assurance and longitudinal follow-up.

**Evidence maturity gate:** For every future feature or claim record (a) precise claim and date, (b) primary source URL and document revision, (c) decision-making authority, (d) announced versus approved versus contracted versus installed versus operational status, (e) baseline and counterfactual, (f) affected populations, (g) privacy/security/accessibility risks, (h) independent validation and competing evidence, and (i) correction and review date. An absence of evidence is **unknown**, not proof of absence.

**Three research horizons:**
1. **Could:** Technical feasibility, architectural dependencies, failure modes, energy and data requirements. Evaluate using open specifications and synthetic scenarios.
2. **Should:** Proportionality, public legitimacy, accessibility, privacy, cybersecurity, labor, inclusion, institutional capacity and environmental tradeoffs. Compare non-digital alternatives.
3. **Does:** Evidence of actual procurement, deployment, operating performance, costs, adverse events and longitudinal outcomes. Require contemporaneous project records before describing any system as deployed.

**Proposed comparative indicators:** assisted versus self-service admission success; queue distribution rather than only averages; false rejection and appeal resolution; wheelchair-accessible routes and device-free alternatives; local jobs and supplier participation; privacy complaints; breach and recovery metrics; public-space use; energy and water intensity; flood resilience; transit reliability; and community satisfaction. Report subgroup outcomes only where lawful, ethical and statistically supportable.

**Falsifiable propositions for future testing:**
- MP-H1: Mobile-only access can lower mean entry time but increase exclusion for visitors without compatible devices; a staffed alternative may mitigate the tradeoff.
- MP-H2: Algorithmic queue optimization can improve venue throughput while increasing external curbside congestion; evaluate venue and neighborhood measures together.
- MP-H3: Centralized identity and service integration can simplify operations but increase the consequences of a single cyber or vendor failure; evaluate modular fallback.
- MP-H4: Robot-assisted services can change labor demand and accessibility in opposing directions; assess role redesign, human assistance and measurable outcomes.
- MP-H5: A leapfrogging strategy is more likely to create broad local value when governance, digital access, resilient utilities and local capability develop alongside the technology; compare alternative institutional explanations.
