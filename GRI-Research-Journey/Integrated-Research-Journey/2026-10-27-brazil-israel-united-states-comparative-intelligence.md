## Continuing inquiry | Authority, correction and learning

Grand Research Institute | Research continuation | October 27, 2026

**Research proposition:** The capacity of a system to learn should be examined alongside the capacity of affected people and institutions to question, correct and limit its decisions. This is a GRI normative proposition and research agenda, not a validated result.

This inquiry continues the existing [institutional power framework](2026-10-19-institutional-power-amplification-framework.md), [living algorithmic governance research](2026-10-21-algorithmic-governance-living-research.md) and [Uber and Lyft studies](../Algorithmic-Management/Uber-Lyft-Research/README.md). It uses the three country cases to investigate authority under different institutional conditions. Courts, electoral administrators, employers and platforms have different mandates and review structures; their comparison concerns decision processes rather than legal equivalence.

### Questions within the existing framework

| Framework stage | Comparative question | Evidence to seek | Algorithmic management connection |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Access | Who can see the evidence, rules and reasons for a consequential decision? | Published orders, decision notices, access restrictions and disclosure dates | Workers and platform users may have limited access to allocation or scoring rules |
| Capability | What can the institution or technical system actually observe and change? | Documented functions, deployment records and implementation scope | Distinguish prediction, recommendation, incentive and restriction |
| Authority | Who authorized intervention, under which rule, and within what limits? | Mandate, jurisdiction, delegation, review route and duration | A technical capability does not itself confer decision rights |
| Consequences | Who received a benefit, burden or different procedural treatment? | Affected units, implementation timing, comparison observations and alternative explanations | Examine allocation, access and economic effects separately from intent |
| Feedback | Can an affected person obtain review, correction and a reasoned response? | Appeals, reversals, remedies, response times and subsequent rule changes | Test whether errors are corrected and whether corrections influence later decisions |

The unit of analysis remains a dated institutional decision or policy event. Country names are contextual attributes, not training labels for democratic quality, political ideology or legitimacy. A court decision is not inherently a machine learning event. Any claim of AI involvement requires separate evidence of a technical mechanism and its actual use.

### Brazil inquiry prompted by the judicial neutrality discussion

The discussion of judicial neutrality supplies a question to investigate, not a label of proven judicial bias. Two primary institutional communications establish a bounded starting point:

1. The [STF communication of October 25, 2026](https://noticias.stf.jus.br/postsnoticias/stf-prorroga-votacao-ate-as-19h-em-locais-onde-urnas-permaneceram-abertas-apos-as-17h/) describes Justice Cristiano Zanin's voting extension, identifies Rcl 101673 and ADPF 1013, and cites transportation complaints from both presidential candidacies.
2. The [TSE clarification of October 25, 2026](https://www.tse.jus.br/comunicacao/noticias/2026/Outubro/esclarecimento-sobre-prorrogacao-da-votacao) states that voting continued only where it had not already closed at 17:00 and that receipt of voting records, totalization and publication of results would resume after 19:00.

These communications document the institutions' stated rationale and operational instructions. They do not independently adjudicate jurisdiction, establish neutral implementation or measure public trust. Review of the underlying orders, petitions, transmission timestamps, affected polling places and subsequent proceedings remains necessary.

Questions for this event are: Was the intervention within the claimed mandate? Were comparable access problems treated consistently? What explains differences in implementation? What review and correction routes were available? Did later institutional changes address documented problems? Election outcome effects, partisan favoritism and loss of public trust each require their own evidence.

Apply these same questions to separately documented Israel and United States events. Do not assign either country an analogous judicial incident without its own source record. The comparison must permit findings of effective restraint, justified intervention and successful correction as well as failures.

### Distinguishing three forms of learning

**Research learning:** New evidence and corrections change the case interpretation, with dated provenance and competing explanations preserved.

**Institutional learning:** A documented review changes an institutional rule, delegation, procedure or remedy. Publication of a report alone does not establish that practice changed.

**Machine learning:** Model parameters change through a specified training process. The existing [country system methods](../Research-Governance/ConsciousAI-Research-Systems/COUNTRY_INTELLIGENCE.md) describe experimental topic suggestions, not automatic retraining, political forecasting or a validated measure of institutional legitimacy. Collection, retrieval and research linking must not be described as model learning.

For a future evaluated model revision, use independently reviewed labels, reviewer disagreement records, source and time separated evaluation, language specific reporting, abstention and versioned approval. Keep related reports about the same event in the same evaluation partition to reduce leakage. Unresolved allegations and model suggestions must not become verified labels through repetition.

### Prospective measures and competing explanations

Measure decision to explanation time, appeal to disposition time, and verified error to correction time separately. Report the number of eligible and observed decisions, missing records and unresolved appeals. A high reversal rate may reflect effective review or poor initial decisions; a low rate may reflect accuracy or inaccessible appeals. Trust requires a separate survey design rather than inference from criticism or compliance.

Two prospective hypotheses can guide the existing evidence review:

1. Accessible reasons and review routes are associated with faster correction of independently identified errors, conditional on case complexity, resources and emergency conditions.
2. Technical capability can expand faster than review capacity, producing an observable gap between intervention and effective contestability.

Neither hypothesis is established by these three country cases. Seek counterexamples and preserve institutional differences before estimating associations or causal effects. The existing synthetic platform models remain methods demonstrations, separate from observed governance outcomes.

### Scholarly and institutional foundations

Kellogg, Valentine and Christin's [Algorithms at Work](https://doi.org/10.5465/annals.2018.0174), *Academy of Management Annals*, 14(1), 366–410 (2020), supplies the organizational control and contestation lens. March's [Exploration and Exploitation in Organizational Learning](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2.1.71), *Organization Science*, 2(1), 71–87 (1991), supplies a foundation for examining organizational adaptation. [NIST AI RMF 1.0](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.100-1) (2023) provides an institutional reference for governing, mapping, measuring and managing AI risks.

Their use here supports conceptual development, not validation of electoral allegations or endorsement of GRI's comparative extension. The Nelson institutional approach already documented in the country cases continues to organize rules, actors, interests, power and adaptation. This contribution is independent GRI research.
