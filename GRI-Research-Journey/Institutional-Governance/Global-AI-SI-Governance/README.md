# Global AI and SI Governance: Institutional Accountability for Autonomous Agents

**Grand Research Institute | Research Journey**  
**Research lead:** David Grand  
**Version:** 1.0 | October 12, 2026  
**Research status:** Living, exploratory comparative governance research

## Research thesis

The emergence of agents acting autonomously across physical, financial, and digital environments places new pressure on existing authorization, audit, jurisdiction, and liability systems. The proposed “Bretton Woods moment” is an analytical analogy for institutional coordination, not a prediction of a single world AI regulator.

## Comparative governance register

| Hub | Principal instrument | Status / relevance |
|---|---|---|
| European Union | EU AI Act; CEN/CENELEC standardization | Binding law with phased obligations; technical harmonized standards support compliance and are not independently equivalent to legislation |
| United States | Sectoral rules and procurement requirements; NIST AI RMF | Diverse agency obligations plus voluntary risk management guidance |
| Singapore | IMDA Model AI Governance Framework for Agentic AI | Voluntary operational guidance on accountable delegation and controls |
| United Nations | Independent International Scientific Panel on AI; Global Dialogue | Scientific assessment and international coordination, not direct enforcement |
| ISO/IEC | ISO/IEC 42001 and ISO/IEC 42006 | AI management system requirements and separate requirements for certification bodies |

**Key standards correction:** ISO/IEC 42001:2023 establishes organizational AI management system requirements. ISO/IEC 42006:2025 governs bodies conducting audit and certification of those systems.

## GRI proposed value policy pillars

1. **Dynamic RACI and delegated authority:** Identify the executive accountable for each agent workflow, permitted actions, approvals, escalation, and revocation.
2. **Continuous authorization boundary testing:** Test agent attempts at unauthorized transactions, privileged changes, filings, tool use, and cascading delegation.
3. **Supply chain and action traceability:** Preserve agent actions, model/version metadata, approvals, upstream vendors, data provenance, and relevant incident records subject to security and privacy requirements.
