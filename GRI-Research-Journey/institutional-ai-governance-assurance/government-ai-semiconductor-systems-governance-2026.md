# GRI | Government AI, Semiconductor Assurance and National Infrastructure

**Grand Research Institute — integrated research agenda | 25 October 2026**

## Institutional scope

GRI is extending the Treasury-origin AI label-verification case into a cross-sector investigation of the Fourth Industrial Revolution: public-sector AI screening and opportunity allocation, infrastructure systems engineering, semiconductor trust boundaries, autonomous mobility, institutional accountability, and the distribution of decision authority. This work connects the GRI ConsciousAI repository, [GRI Institutional AI Governance](https://github.com/BazeletDave/GRI-Institutional-AI-Governance), and the [Treasury TTB Label Verification prototype](https://github.com/BazeletDave/Treasury-TTB-Label-Verification). These are related independent research resources, not claims of government affiliation.

**Central GRI hypothesis:** AI capabilities and infrastructure may scale faster than institutions can verify data, govern delegation, contest consequential classifications, and correct errors. This is a research hypothesis, not a validated universal law.

**Critical distinction:** Chip-level confidentiality, attestation and isolation are **security safeguards**, not proofs of model accuracy, freedom from bias, civil-rights compliance, safe driving or legally authorized decisions. Tesla's planned compute improvements are manufacturer targets, not independently demonstrated safety outcomes. Hardware safety claims require system-level evidence.

## Proposed assurance architecture

1. **Silicon and firmware:** Hardware identity, secure boot where supported, attestation, firmware provenance, isolation, fault containment and physical/thermal reliability.
2. **System software:** Trusted workload launch, signed images, version-pinned dependencies, access control, secure logging, key custody and recovery.
3. **Models and data:** Rights-cleared data provenance, representative evaluations, error rates, distribution shift, bias and proxy analysis, model change control.
4. **Agent authority:** Capability scopes, policy enforcement independent of model output, human approvals, revocation, transaction limits and traceable overrides.
5. **Institutional legitimacy:** Statutory authority, procurement accountability, transparency, contestability, accessible appeals, independent review and remedies.
6. **Learning feedback:** Record incidents and near misses, update controls, re-test across jurisdictions and publish bounded findings.

## Testable propositions (not findings)

- **P5:** More capable AI infrastructure without commensurate decision contestability can increase the scale of uncorrected institutional errors.
- **P6:** Hardware-rooted attestation improves evidence of execution-environment integrity but cannot by itself validate decision fairness or accuracy.
- **P7:** Separating predictive recommendations from authorized government determinations increases traceability of responsibility.
- **P8:** Autonomous-system safety depends on integrated hardware, software, human and institutional controls rather than compute performance alone.

## Institutional causal research extension | 2 November 2026

**Research status:** Prospective comparative design. The original October 25 framework, six assurance layers and propositions P5–P8 remain unchanged. This section defines falsifiable mechanisms and competing explanations rather than asserting that AI already outpaces all institutional oversight.

### Causal model and units of analysis

**Central question:** Under what conditions does AI infrastructure expansion increase public value while preserving verifiable, contestable institutional authority?

**Units:** agency by year; procurement or deployment by release; country by sector by year; and consequential decision workflow by cohort. Do not pool unlike units or infer hiring outcomes from repository forks.

**Proposed mechanism:** increased compute and model availability -> faster and broader deployment -> increased delegation and technical dependence -> pressure on verification, contestability and remediation capacity -> divergent public value and legitimacy outcomes. Institutional investment, statutory safeguards, local expertise and independent audit can moderate or reverse this pathway. Each arrow requires evidence and possible disconfirmation.

### Measurement and falsification matrix

| Construct | Proposed observable measure | Rival explanation / falsification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capability expansion | documented compute availability, model releases, procurement and production deployment dates | vendor announcements may precede or never produce operational capability |
| Institutional verification | fraction of deployments with reproducible evaluations, traceable data and independent access | improved audit capability may keep pace with deployment |
| Delegation control | percentage of consequential actions with scoped authority, logged approval, revocation and override | nominal human review may not constitute effective control |
| Contestability | notice coverage, accessible appeal rate, review latency and reversal or correction rate | low appeal rates can reflect low error, barriers to appeal or low awareness |
| Institutional dependence | supplier concentration, switching costs, continuity exercises, public technical staffing | specialization can increase capability without reducing effective authority |
| Leapfrogging and inclusion | local compute access, connectivity, power reliability, workforce skills and retained domestic value | growth may be explained by baseline infrastructure, financing or sector composition |

Record numerator, denominator, cohort, jurisdiction, period, issuer, source URL, extraction date, uncertainty and missingness. A higher correction rate alone cannot be interpreted as deterioration without exposure and detection rates. Where valid comparison groups are available, use longitudinal or matched cases, sensitivity analysis and negative cases; do not claim causal identification from descriptive correlations.

### Candidate empirical comparisons and ethical safeguards

1. **Treasury/TTB:** distinguish the publicly supplied engineering scenario from authenticated federal procurement, workforce, recruitment and deployed systems. Candidate funnel denominators, AI screening and hiring outcomes remain unknown until documented.
2. **NVIDIA:** compare publicly documented semiconductor architecture and confidential computing claims with independent verification of deployment, supply and energy constraints.
3. **Tesla:** distinguish development targets from demonstrated autonomous safety and operational oversight.
4. **Africa and leapfrogging:** select country-sector cases only after comparable data and counterexamples are identified; test both enabling capacity and dependency risks.
5. **GRI algorithmic management:** reuse consented field evidence and synthetic tests, without generalizing from a single platform or sample.

**Decision gate:** require primary-source traceability, construct validity, rival-hypothesis assessment, ethical review of sensitive recruitment data, and independent reproduction before elevating any proposition to an empirical finding. No new project, continuous collection, automated model training, or dashboard is authorized.

**Research contribution sought:** a replicable institutional adaptation and assurance comparison explaining when technical acceleration improves public value and when it increases unaccountable dependence. This is an intended contribution, not a demonstrated result.
