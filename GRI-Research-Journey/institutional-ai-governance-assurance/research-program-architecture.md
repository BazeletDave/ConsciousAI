# GRI Institutional AI Governance | Comparative Research Architecture

**Status:** Research design, not a completed empirical study. **Maintainer:** Grand Research Institute (GRI). **Date:** October 2026.

## Central research problem

How does the expansion of algorithmic and agentic decision capability change the distribution of authority, incentives, accountability, and contestability across public institutions, private platforms, and human participants?

The **AI Governance Paradox** is a GRI working hypothesis: technical capability and adoption may advance faster than the institutional arrangements needed to authorize, supervise, challenge, and remediate consequential decisions. This proposition requires empirical testing; it is not an established universal law.

## Comparative analytical framework

1. **Authority:** Who grants authority, with what scope and revocation rights?
2. **Incentives:** What metrics shape behavior, and which harms might remain unmeasured?
3. **Evidence:** What is observed directly, reported by participants, inferred, or simulated?
4. **Oversight:** What approvals, independent checks, appeal channels, and correction paths exist?
5. **Institutional context:** Compare liberal, economic nationalist, and structuralist lenses as taught by Roy C. Nelson at Thunderbird School of Global Management at Arizona State University; distinguish this teaching framework from GRI's independent hypotheses.
6. **Adaptation:** How do governance arrangements respond to errors, drift, and capability changes?

## Testable propositions (not findings)

- **P1:** Higher decision automation without corresponding contestability mechanisms is associated with lower perceived procedural legitimacy.
- **P2:** Incentive systems optimized for narrow measurable outputs are more likely to leave material stakeholder costs unmeasured unless independent safeguards exist.
- **P3:** Explicit agent capabilities, approval checkpoints, and tamper-evident logs improve the traceability of delegated decisions relative to informal delegation.
- **P4:** Evidence of configured software versions alone is insufficient to establish that active production runtimes have been remediated.

Each proposition needs an operational definition, comparison group, data collection plan, and falsification criterion before empirical claims can be made.

## Boundaries

This is independent GRI research, not a Treasury, Thunderbird, Uber, Lyft, or other institutional endorsement. Do not represent illustrative interviews, synthetic events, prototype scores, or hypothetical deployments as verified external facts. Do not publish private participant data or confidential implementation details.
