1. Executive Assessment
The doctoral journey has progressed beyond a collection of AI observations. It now contains a coherent research program
with a central paradox, a governance framework, an empirical laboratory, a longitudinal evidence method, and a growing
set of higher order questions concerning agency, identity, legitimacy, and ambient intelligence.

The strongest doctoral contribution is not a generic responsible AI framework. It is the
governance problem created when technically compliant AI encounters human
systems whose actual functioning depends on context, adaptation, informal
institutions, competing rules, and judgment.

Primary doctoral question
How should AI enabled systems remain compliant, adaptive, sustainable, and accountable when they operate
across dynamic and sometimes conflicting legal, institutional, environmental, cultural, and human systems, and
when AI increasingly mediates human agency, identity, and perception?
The question should remain broad enough to preserve the general theory while mobility provides the bounded empirical
environment in which the constructs can be observed and tested.

3. Adaptive AI Governance Framework
The current framework evaluates AI through six intelligence layers and culminates in a Responsible Outcome assessment.
This is the bridge between abstract governance and observable system performance.
LEGAL What is permitted? Law, standards, liability, operational constraints
BEHAVIORAL What do people actually do? Adaptation, fatigue, risk compensation, reliance
ETHICAL What should be protected? Dignity, fairness, autonomy, access, human agency

GOVERNANCE Who can explain and correct it? Accountability, auditability, oversight, contestability
Evidence classification protocol
Every major claim should now be tagged as one of four classes: Established Literature, Primary Evidence, GRI
Proposition, or Empirical Validation Required. Simulations must remain explicitly separated from real world deployment
evidence.
This protocol directly addresses the major weakness identified in earlier versions of the monograph: strong conceptual
originality accompanied by insufficient separation among observation, simulation, inference, and theory.

4. Mobility as the Empirical Laboratory
Mobility remains the strongest bounded setting because AI decisions encounter formal law, informal behavior, safety
constraints, infrastructure, economic incentives, and human adaptation simultaneously.

Uber AV Labs Human driving becomes geographically diverse training infrastructure for autonomous systems. Human behavior to data to machine adaptation

Central mobility proposition
An autonomous system can achieve increasingly accurate perception while remaining constrained by contextual
competence. The governance challenge therefore shifts from whether AI can detect reality to whether it can
interpret and act within locally constructed systems of human behavior.
This proposition creates a direct connection between autonomous mobility and institutional political economy. A vehicle
entering a new jurisdiction enters not only a different road network, but a different institutional environment.

6. Scholarly Grounding: What the External Evidence Already
Supports
The conceptual direction is consistent with major governance institutions, but the doctoral contribution must remain
distinguishable from them.
NIST
The NIST AI Risk Management Framework recognizes that mathematical representation of complex human phenomena
can remove necessary context. It also requires clear differentiation of human and AI roles and treats governance as
continuous across the AI lifecycle. This provides authoritative support for the importance of context and human machine
configuration, but it does not resolve the specific GRI paradox concerning formal compliance versus situated adaptation.
OECD
The OECD AI Principles connect trustworthy AI with human autonomy, oversight, transparency, robustness, safety,
accountability, traceability, lifecycle risk management, inclusive growth, and environmental sustainability. This strongly
supports the multidimensional character of the GRI framework while leaving room for a distinct theory of adaptive
governance across conflicting institutional environments.
United States Department of Transportation
Federal automated vehicle research explicitly includes human factors, driver vehicle interfaces, cybersecurity, functional
safety, and testable automated driving scenarios. These provide primary anchors for the mobility research design and help
move the work from conceptual illustration toward measurable transportation research.
Implication: GRI should not claim novelty for context, accountability, human oversight, or lifecycle risk
management by themselves. Novelty must be demonstrated in the integration of compliance, contextual
adaptation, institutional variation, responsible outcomes, and the transition toward agency, identity, and
ambient intelligence.

7. Evidence Gaps and ASU Library Pull Protocol
The next phase is an evidence audit. The objective is not to add citations everywhere. It is to locate the strongest
scholarship for each construct and identify where the GRI theory genuinely extends existing knowledge.
Priority Evidence need Action
1 Peer reviewed literature on contextual AI, human automation, institutional theory, human machine teaming, and adaptive governance. Search accessible scholarship first. Flag restricted full text for ASU retrieval.
2 Formal construct definitions and measurement precedents.Build a construct dictionary with source, definition, variable, scale, and limitations.
3 Mobility safety and human factors evidence. Prioritize NHTSA, USDOT, peer reviewed transportation research, and platform safety publications.
4 Cross jurisdiction institutional comparison. Build a structured comparison of formal rules, informal norms, operational design domains, and governance regimes.
5 Agency, identity, provenance, and ambient intelligence literature. Establish what is already known before advancing GRI propositions.
6 Reproducibility and provenance. Maintain versioned evidence registers, source archive, claim audit, and explicit simulation labels.
Standing ASU protocol
When a high value source is restricted, the research record should specify the database, exact title, author, journal or
publisher, year, DOI when available, and the precise reason it is needed. The user then retrieves the source through
authenticated ASU access and returns the PDF or export for analysis. A weaker substitute should not be used merely
because it is easier to access.
