## The research proposition

Artificial intelligence changes more than organizational efficiency. It changes how authority is delegated, how labor is governed, how evidence is authenticated, and how institutions retain control over decisions executed by automated systems. The central GRI question is:

**How can management and public institutions preserve meaningful human agency, accountability, and legitimacy as software moves from advising decisions to executing them?**

The Research Journey traces the GRI research program from 2021 onward and connects multiple years of applied industry experience, management education, writing, observational research, and digital experimentation. Its central contribution is a **comparative research architecture**, not a claim that every proposed hypothesis has already been validated.

## Selected empirical finding | Platform earnings

The October 7, 2026 GRI Uber and Lyft evidence dossier documents **three displayed ride offers**, totaling **88 displayed trip minutes** and **$30.12 in offered compensation**. The time weighted equivalent is **$20.54 per displayed trip hour**, or **$19.79** after removing the one identifiable **$1.10** bonus. These are **offer screen equivalents, not realized hourly wages**. They exclude driver online time, waiting, repositioning, operating costs, and tips.

The data motivate a testable question about algorithmic compensation patterns. **Three offers do not establish a deliberate $20 wage target, a platform wide policy, or a causal mechanism.** Further data collection and preregistered analysis are necessary.

## Current research case | Agentic commerce and financial authority (October 2026)

**[Agentic Commerce and Financial Authority: A Bretton Woods Analogy for AI Governance](../Institutional-Governance/Agentic-Commerce-Financial-Authority/)** examines the public October 10 to 12, 2026 Grok credit card photograph proposal and LEGO ordering demonstration. The working case differentiates confirmed statements, reported order detail exposure, disputed transaction interference, unresolved technical safeguards, and a proposed synthetic comparative experiment.

Its **Bretton Woods analogy is explicitly a testable institutional coordination proposition**, not a claim of equivalent historical significance. The study extends GRI's existing authorization, provenance, algorithmic management and institutional governance research to payment authority, interplatform accountability and cross-border rule formation.

## A unified research architecture

Three empirical pillars anchor the continuing research program:

1. **Algorithmic management:** How platforms allocate opportunities, measure performance, and exercise managerial control through software.
2. **Agentic management:** How operational authority, audit trails, human approvals, and exception handling change when software agents execute work.
3. **Institutional governance:** How organizations coordinate authority and provenance across jurisdictional, administrative, and cultural boundaries.

These studies are connected through institutional theory, organizational control, comparative political economy, and the governance of AI. The proposed integration asks:

> **What changes when algorithmic systems exercise authority over people, compared with systems in which transparent rules constrain the authority of management itself?**

## Research quality and safeguards

GRI distinguishes **primary evidence**, **documented observations**, **interpretations**, **testable propositions**, **prototypes**, and **future inquiry**. Reviews should evaluate the quality of evidence and reasoning, not simply the breadth of topics.

Priority standards are peer reviewed management and institutional scholarship, primary governmental and regulatory sources, original corporate disclosures, replicable calculations, provenance trails, and explicit study limitations. Field research involving passengers or sensitive records must be deidentified and reviewed before public release.
