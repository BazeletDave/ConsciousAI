# Institutional AI Gatekeeping: From Engineering Evaluation to Public Accountability

**Grand Research Institute | Living AI Ecosystem | 2 November 2026**  
**Status:** Exploratory, evidence bounded extension of existing Institutional AI Governance and AI Paradox research. Not a new research program or assurance certification.

## Research question
When public institutions use AI to recruit technical talent, evaluate submissions, administer regulatory decisions, or deliver services, who retains effective authority to recognize merit, explain decisions, correct errors, and provide meaningful human review?

## Original engineering evidence
The publicly accessible [treasurytakehome-rgb/instructions README](https://github.com/treasurytakehome-rgb/instructions/blob/main/README.md) describes an AI powered alcohol label verification take home prototype, source repository and deployed application deliverables, and qualitative engineering evaluation criteria. Its named stakeholder interviews and quantitative assertions are **exercise supplied scenarios, not independently authenticated Treasury testimony or operational statistics**. Public availability does not establish government authorship, an official hiring relationship, candidate outcomes, or production deployment. Preserve source URL, retrieval date, and commit SHA before treating this as immutable evidence.

The screenshot of the account showed six GitHub contributions in a year and 61 forks of the instructions repository. Contributions are not people; forks are not applicants, completed submissions, successful candidates, or hires. No success ratio is calculable without verified cohort and selection counts.

## Independent institutional evidence
* [TTB certificate of label approval regulatory framework](https://www.ttb.gov/alfd/certificate-of-label-aproval-cola) describes actual federal label regulation and the COLA process.
* [TTB COLAs Online FAQ](https://www.ttb.gov/faqs/colas-and-formulas-online-faqs) documents confidentiality of pending applications, human involvement in review, and correction pathways. This does not validate the exercise's fictionalized interview claims.
* [TTB FY 2027 Congressional Justification](https://www.ttb.gov/system/files/2026-04/TTB-FY-2027-CJ.pdf) reports nearly 163,000 label applications in FY 2025, a separate government reporting population.
* [GAO 26 107522](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-107522) identifies IRS AI workforce skills, inventory, and strategic management gaps. This is a comparative Treasury bureau case, not evidence of TTB hiring outcomes.
* [GAO 25 107933](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-25-107933) describes government wide AI oversight requirements and institutional roles.
* [GAO 25 107126](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-25-107126) surveys research on digital surveillance and employment implications.

## Testable propositions
**P1: Gatekeeping.** Where automated hiring or submission evaluation is actually documented, measure screening stages, human review access, notice, appeal and error correction. Do not assume that this take home exercise was evaluated by AI.

**P2: Institutional capacity.** Compare the rate and scope of technical adoption with documented workforce, oversight, procurement and audit capability, preserving agency, year and population denominators.

**P3: Formal versus effective authority.** Separate statutory decision authority, control of technical infrastructure, and demonstrated ability to inspect, override, or reverse AI influenced decisions.

**P4: Cross sector comparison.** Use Treasury and TTB as public administration context; NVIDIA for AI compute infrastructure; Tesla for autonomy and safety; and major model providers for platform governance. Compare only equivalent, sourced constructs. Do not infer governmental capture, unlawful conduct, or direct presidential influence from commercial scale or executive access.

## Candidate funnel evidence register
Collect separately: job posting and hiring authority; number of applicants; invitations to take home assessment; completed submissions; review rubric; human versus automated screening; interviews; offers; hires; appeals. Each field requires source, time window, population, access classification, and validation status. Unknown values remain **not available**, never zero. No applicant identifying information should be published.

2. Separate primary agency records, independent oversight reports, repository observations, stakeholder scenarios, and research hypotheses.
3. Test technical claims only on authorized or synthetic artifacts. Preserve reproducibility logs and exact commit references.
4. Require independent review before labeling any institutional effect demonstrated.
5. Apply the existing GRI AI Paradox, institutional frameworks, algorithmic management and Tiger Assurance methods; no new dashboard, autonomous collection, or program.

## Interpretation
The hypothesis is that the pace of technical capability may exceed the capacity of institutions to maintain effective, contestable oversight. This is not a finding that AI is above the law, that particular executives govern public institutions, or that any identified candidate was rejected by an automated system. The societal benefit criterion is accountable human authority over consequential AI mediated decisions.
