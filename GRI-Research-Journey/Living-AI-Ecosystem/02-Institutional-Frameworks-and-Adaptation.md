# Institutional Frameworks and Adaptation

**GRI | Comparative conceptual research note | October 16, 2026**

## Research premise
Institutional responses to AI should be studied through recognized theories of rules, incentives, organizational authority and political economy, rather than assuming that a new technology requires an entirely new institution.

## Established scholarly foundations
- **Douglass C. North:** institutions as formal and informal rules shaping incentives and organizational behavior. *Institutions, Institutional Change and Economic Performance* (1990).
- **W. Richard Scott:** regulative, normative and cultural cognitive dimensions of institutional analysis. *Institutions and Organizations* (4th ed., 2014).
- **Elinor Ostrom:** institutional arrangements and collective governance, including rule design and accountability. *Governing the Commons* (1990).
- **Kellogg, Valentine and Christin:** algorithmic systems and contested organizational control. *Academy of Management Annals* 14(1), 2020, doi:10.5465/annals.2018.0174.

GRI's rideshare decision illustrations present a simplified chain: **input → prediction → judgment → action → outcome → feedback**. Governance spans all stages, not only the final decision. Feedback is not equivalent to automatic retraining, and human oversight must be specified in operational rather than purely rhetorical terms.

## GRI BZLT Americas FLA Institutional | Florida medical governance and federal recognition (31 October 2026)

### Analytical center: compliance and independent assurance

**Primary question:** Across Florida's evolving medical licensing regime and the contrasting adult use regulatory model, which institutions have enforceable authority, what evidence demonstrates compliance, who independently verifies that evidence, and what happens when a control fails? This reframes the case from cannabis market commentary into an institutional assurance test.

**Historical case: Florida medical licensing.** Trace the 2014 limited program, 2015 license selection, 2016 constitutional medical authorization, 2017 MMTC implementation, subsequent litigation, and the separately scoped 2026 federal action. For each decision record the governing legal version, issuing authority, licensed legal entity, operating authorization, duty, control evidence, review mechanism and available remedy.

**Comparison: recreational regulation.** Florida's 2024 adult use constitutional proposal failed the required 60 percent threshold; therefore Florida does not provide a legal recreational retail market against which to compare realized state sales. Compare Florida's *nonauthorization and attempted constitutional change* with documented adult use frameworks in other jurisdictions only after selecting jurisdictions with verifiable statutory and regulator data. Separate ballot votes, lawful operators, revenue, enforcement, safety and tax receipts; never treat them as interchangeable denominators.

**Minimum evidence schema:** `jurisdiction | effective_date | institution | legal_authority | entity_id | permission | control_objective | evidence_record | reviewer | exception | remediation | evidence_status | source_url`. Classify each entry as verified primary record, attributed secondary report, company claim, hypothesis or unknown. A license, registration, policy statement or algorithmic test flag alone is **not** an independent certification.

**Corporate and economic testing:** Build a dated legal entity ownership crosswalk for Trulieve, Curaleaf, Verano/MÜV and comparators. Only compare like denominators, and separately label MMTC license counts, retail locations, patients, product quantities, consolidated company revenue, Florida attributable revenue, government fees and tax effects. Test whether incumbency predicts footprint or financing advantages, controlling where feasible for timing, acquisition and regulatory change. No causal claim without evidence and alternative explanations.

**Assurance network implication:** Reuse existing GRI governance and Tiger Assurance concepts of explicit authorization, provenance, revocation, independently reproducible controls, incident escalation and correction. This section defines a *research control matrix*, not a deployed compliance platform, external audit opinion or regulator endorsement. No new repository, monitoring pipeline or confidential disclosure.

### Historical and comparative evidence


**Scope:** A bounded comparative extension of the existing institutional adaptation and driver impairment scholarship, not a separate cannabis project, an automated monitoring stream, or an assertion of clinical or financial outcomes.

### Institutional sequence and legal distinctions

* **2014:** Florida's Compassionate Medical Cannabis Act established a limited low THC program and a restricted dispensing organization framework. Examine the original nursery eligibility criteria and regional selection mechanism in the enacted statute, rather than treating contemporary corporate brands as the original legal licensees.
* **2015:** Florida selected initial dispensing organizations. Reconstruct the official license orders, legal entities, subsequent transfers and ownership history before drawing conclusions about incumbent advantage.
* **2016:** Florida voters adopted Amendment 2, extending constitutional medical marijuana access for qualifying patients. Constitutional authorization did not remove state implementation and licensing requirements.
* **2017 onward:** Florida implemented the expanded MMTC framework under Fla. Stat. §381.986, including vertical integration. Review *Florida Department of Health v. Florigrown, LLC* (Florida Supreme Court, 2021) when analyzing the constitutional and statutory relationship.
* **30 October 2026:** DOJ announced initial DEA federal registrations for **more than 900 dispensaries across seven states**, including Florida, under an April 23 federal order applying Schedule III controls to defined qualifying medical marijuana activities. The DOJ release does **not** name Florida recipients or provide a Florida approval count. State licensure alone is not DEA registration. Recreational activities are excluded; other marijuana activities remain subject to their applicable federal controls.

**Research propositions, not findings:** (1) initial licensing rules may create persistent entry advantages; (2) new federal registration may change the economics of existing licenses; (3) authorization by one institution does not demonstrate safety, access, competition or accountability across the full system. Test each proposition with longitudinal primary records and explicit counterfactual limitations.

**Financial evidence design:** Trace (a) legal MMTC license holder → operating subsidiary → public parent; (b) acquisitions and financing; (c) Florida dispensing footprint by date; (d) reported consolidated versus Florida attributable revenue; (e) verified tax and banking changes. Trulieve, Curaleaf and Verano are **research subjects**, not confirmed federal registrants. Do not present applications as approvals, dispensary counts as market share, or consolidated revenue as Florida revenue. Suggested future exhibits are a sourced regulatory timeline, an authority matrix and a historical ownership/financial-flow chart; do not activate a live pipeline.

**Impairment and ZERO connection:** Medical eligibility, THC detection, clinically demonstrated therapeutic efficacy and fitness to drive are separate propositions. Review the existing [driver safety research](../../DriverREADME.md) and the ZERO evidence register before citing laboratory or field performance claims. Any impairment model requires independent labels, measured error rates, subgroup assessment, human contestability and consent safeguards. This regulatory event alone is not evidence that driving safety improved or worsened.

**Verification and disclosure guardrails:** Distinguish official records from journalism, corporate disclosures and hypotheses. The original GRI driver document contains unverified research assertions and placeholder-looking references; do not elevate those to empirical findings without primary study records. ### Integrated institutional outcomes: money, health, safety and legitimacy

This extension applies the same **authority → compliance duty → evidence → independent review → exception → remedy** sequence to four outcome domains. It is a research protocol, not a finding that any institution or company failed a duty.

| Domain | Unit of analysis | Candidate measures | Principal evidence | Assurance failure to test |

#### Financial tracing and comparative design

* Create a time indexed corporate entity map for Trulieve, Curaleaf and Verano/MÜV: **parent → subsidiary → Florida MMTC license → locations → dated filings**. Preserve original license names and documented ownership changes.
* Distinguish stock market capitalization, capital raised, acquisition consideration, operating revenue, gross margin, taxes, compliance expenditure and patient spending. These measure different financial flows.
* For each company/year, record source document, currency, reporting perimeter, accounting standard and whether Florida specific figures are disclosed. Unknown figures remain unknown, never inferred from store counts without a labeled model and sensitivity analysis.
* Use concentration ratios or Herfindahl Hirschman Index only with a consistent dated numerator and denominator. A concentration index based on dispensary locations is **location concentration**, not revenue market concentration.
* For adult use comparators, predefine selection criteria (authorized retail system, available primary financial/regulatory reports, comparable years). Compare regulatory architectures first; avoid direct per capita revenue comparisons without adjusting for population, taxes, tourism, medical overlap and reporting definitions.

#### Interpretation

The central GRI proposition is that **regulatory authorization, organizational compliance, independently verifiable assurance and beneficial societal outcomes are four different states**. The Florida history provides a longitudinal case of legal authority and market formation; other jurisdictions' authorized adult use markets provide a comparison of regulatory architecture. Evidence of compliance should be assessed without assuming causation, safety, equitable access or legitimacy.
