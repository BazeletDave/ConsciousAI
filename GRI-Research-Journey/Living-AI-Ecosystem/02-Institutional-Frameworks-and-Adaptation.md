# Institutional Frameworks and Adaptation

**GRI | Comparative conceptual research note | October 16, 2026**

## Research premise
Institutional responses to AI should be studied through recognized theories of rules, incentives, organizational authority and political economy, rather than assuming that a new technology requires an entirely new institution.

## Established scholarly foundations
- **Douglass C. North:** institutions as formal and informal rules shaping incentives and organizational behavior. *Institutions, Institutional Change and Economic Performance* (1990).
- **W. Richard Scott:** regulative, normative and cultural cognitive dimensions of institutional analysis. *Institutions and Organizations* (4th ed., 2014).
- **Elinor Ostrom:** institutional arrangements and collective governance, including rule design and accountability. *Governing the Commons* (1990).
- **Kellogg, Valentine and Christin:** algorithmic systems and contested organizational control. *Academy of Management Annals* 14(1), 2020, doi:10.5465/annals.2018.0174.

## Technology and adaptability
A supplied October 2026 diagram titled *Understanding the graph* depicts technology change rising faster than institutional adaptability. Its caption identifies it as a **conceptual illustration**, not a measured time series or forecast. It motivates a testable question: under what organizational and jurisdictional conditions does the pace of capability deployment exceed the pace of effective governance adaptation?

## Decision governance
GRI's rideshare decision illustrations present a simplified chain: **input → prediction → judgment → action → outcome → feedback**. Governance spans all stages, not only the final decision. Feedback is not equivalent to automatic retraining, and human oversight must be specified in operational rather than purely rhetorical terms.

## Comparative cases
Possible domains include platform labor, agentic commerce, mobile device information exposure, critical infrastructure and public sector AI procurement. Cross-case comparison should distinguish legal requirements, voluntary standards, operational practice, observed results and research hypotheses.

## Research boundary
This collection applies established institutional theories to contemporary AI questions. It neither attributes original GRI propositions to named scholars nor represents GRI as a formal standards body.
