## Research proposition

An increase in roadway sensing and validation capacity does not itself establish a corresponding increase in independent institutional capacity to verify safety, assign responsibility, and contest decisions. This is a falsifiable GRI hypothesis, not a finding established by the observed vehicles.

## Institutional analytical connection

Use the existing GRI AI Paradox, algorithmic management, autonomous mobility, institutional governance and Tiger Assurance frameworks to compare technical capability, operational authority, public oversight, and independent verification. The Florida observation is a case input, not a representative sample or evidence of a commercial rollout.

Alternative explanations include mobile mapping, sensor calibration, camera reference collection, and supervised development operations. The observation does not determine ownership, exact sensor type, device communication content or autonomy status.

Evidence capable of weakening the institutional lag hypothesis would include transparent applicable authorization, independently reproducible safety results, documented human intervention procedures and verified performance within a defined operational design domain. Lack of public access to evidence does not prove the absence of internal controls.

### Verified institutional sources and limits

1. **Florida Statutes §316.85 (2026)** addresses operation and testing of autonomous vehicles and permits fully autonomous vehicles to operate without a human physically present under specified law. The presence of a roof sensor does not establish that an automated driving system was engaged. Primary text: https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm/Ch0825/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&Search_String=&URL=0300-0399%2F0316%2FSections%2F0316.85.html

It is not evidence of the registration or compliance status of the observed vehicles. Primary text: https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/319.145
3. **Florida's Turnpike Enterprise permits guidance** says work on its system or in some circumstances adjacent to its right of way may require an FDOT permit or construction agreement depending on the activity. Ordinary vehicle travel is not necessarily such work; no permit requirement or approval for the observed vehicles is established. Source: https://floridasturnpike.com/business-opportunities/permits/

Sources: https://www.fdot.gov/geospatial/doc-pubs.shtm and https://www.fdot.gov/docs/default-source/geospatial/documentsandpubs/SurveyandMappingHandbook.pdf

Primary source: https://www.tesla.com/robotaxi/safety

### Federal technical and safety assurance boundary

**NHTSA Standing General Order (SGO) on crash reporting:** NHTSA distinguishes automated driving systems (ADS, SAE Levels 3–5) from Level 2 advanced driver assistance systems (ADAS). It requires specified reporting entities to report qualifying crashes according to engagement and incident criteria. A vehicle's visible hardware does not demonstrate ADS engagement. NHTSA explicitly cautions that publicly reported crash counts are not normalized by fleet exposure or operational design domain and should not be used alone to rank safety across operators. Primary source: https://www.nhtsa.gov/es/node/103486 ; legal order: https://www.nhtsa.gov/sites/nhtsa.gov/files/2025-04/third-amended-SGO-2021-01_2025.pdf
