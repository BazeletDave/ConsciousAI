# The Anatomy of AI Decision Making
**GRI Research Journey | Living dossier v1.0 | 15 October 2026**

## Thesis
AI predictions become organizational decisions only through objectives, judgment, constraints and action. Evaluate decision quality, accountability, and distribution of benefits and burdens, not prediction accuracy alone.

## Decision cycle
1. **Input:** Data provenance, privacy, completeness and timeliness.
2. **Prediction:** Demand, duration or acceptance estimates, uncertainty and calibration.
3. **Judgment:** Objectives, tradeoffs, safety rules and authorization; may be embedded in policy.
4. **Action:** Matching, dispatch, price, incentive or recommendation.
5. **Outcome:** Earnings, service, costs, safety, satisfaction and unintended consequences.
6. **Feedback:** Evaluate actual outcomes; data collection does not imply automatic model retraining.

## Uber application
This is a **conceptual analytical mapping, not a description verified against Uber's internal systems**. Study trip offers, location and timing, acceptance, earnings, expenses, cancellations, and feedback. Differentiate observed platform behavior from inferred model logic. Driver trip selection may be rational adaptation, not necessarily misconduct.

### Core question
When an algorithmic platform becomes more efficient, who receives the gains and who bears the costs: the platform, drivers, riders or communities?

### Data protocol
Record source, timestamp, sampling frame, variable definition, access rights, missingness, privacy controls and limitations. Compare gross online-hour earnings, active-hour earnings, and net earnings after expenses. Exploratory South Florida observations near $20 gross per hour in certain periods **do not prove an intentional earnings target**.

## Data readiness
**Trust and governance:** source reliability, content accuracy, accessibility, security and privacy, validity.
**Content and structure:** richness, consistency, currency and timeliness, granularity, relevancy.

## Learning and management
Descriptive analytics asks what happened; predictive asks what may happen; prescriptive asks what should be done, subject to objectives and constraints. AI workforce readiness requires both literacy and responsible adoption in core operations. Collaboration, judgment, curiosity and critical thinking are nurtured over time.

### Lecture synthesis: four takeaways
1. Learning is lifelong.
2. AI transforms work, not only tasks.
3. Better AI does not automatically produce better decisions.
4. Human capabilities remain critical.

## GRI research questions
- How do platform dispatch and pricing relate to drivers' net earnings?
- How does strategic acceptance behavior relate to outcomes after accounting for selection?
- Which system decisions can be independently observed?
- What oversight improves accuracy, contestability, equity and accountability?

**Unverified hypotheses:** Increased operational efficiency need not increase worker welfare proportionally; stronger data governance may improve accountability.
