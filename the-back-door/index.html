01 / Research framework

Three lenses. One governance problem.

The laboratory uses fictional scenarios to formulate research questions. Claims about real-world systems require independent evidence, provenance and validation.

PILLAR 01

AI Compliance Paradox

How can individually rule-compliant systems interact to produce outcomes that no institution intended or adequately supervised?

PILLAR 02

Algorithmic & Agentic Management

How do dispatch, optimization, incentives and delegated AI actions influence human behavior, discretion and operational authority?

PILLAR 03

Institutional Governance

Where do oversight, contestability, provenance, authorization and accountability reside across interacting organizations and systems?

02 / Fictional teaching case

Hurricane response: when local optimization becomes systemic risk

Imagine a fictional hurricane approaching the Florida Keys. Transportation, emergency messaging, power distribution and automated logistics each optimize against their own operational rules.

No individual system is assumed to be malfunctioning. Yet the combined decisions may concentrate traffic, delay evacuations or distribute resources inequitably. The question is not simply whether each algorithm followed policy—but whether the overall response remained governable.

Scenario status: hypothetical, illustrative and unvalidated. This is not an account of a real incident, operational recommendation or emergency forecast.

Discussion prompts
  • Which decisions require human approval before execution?
  • What evidence would distinguish a coordination failure from a model failure?
  • Who has authority to halt or override interacting systems?
  • How would affected people contest an automated decision?
  • What outcome metrics would reveal unintended harm?

03 / Research method

From thought experiment to testable governance question

1. Frame the system boundary

Identify actors, institutions, decision rights, system objectives and affected populations.

2. Map algorithmic decisions

Document inputs, optimization criteria, dependencies, delegated actions and human override points.

3. Define policy and evidence gates

Specify provenance, permissions, approval requirements, contestability and audit logging before experiments.

4. Compare governed and ungoverned scenarios

Pre-register measures such as false approvals, intervention rates, response latency and adverse outcomes using clearly labeled synthetic data.

5. Review limitations and publish responsibly

Keep simulated findings separate from empirical evidence; require human review before publication or downstream model training.
